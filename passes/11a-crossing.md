## The integral identity

**Source statement.** Write $P(E)$ for the number of pairs of finite subsets $(I,J)$ of $\mathbb N$ with $|I|=|J|$ and total energy $\sum_{i\in I}(2i+1)+\sum_{j\in J}(2j+1)\le E$. Let $\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2$ be nonempty classes of fixed size and fixed energy $e_1,e_2$. The alphabets at $a,c$ are $\Sigma_1$, and those at $b,d$ are $\Sigma_2$. Write $\Theta=2(e_1+e_2)$. Proposition B.2 gives one integral family $\Psi_0,\ldots,\Psi_\Theta$ with sum $[a=c][b=d]$. Each entry uses the four symbol labels. Each summand has two integer product decompositions. [B.2, pp. 22–23](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=22) · [Exact quantifiers and party order](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Identity.lean#L699)

| Cut of that same $\Psi_N$ | Maximum number of products | Hubs in B.3 | Asymptotic hub weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\{a,b\}\mid\{c,d\}$, part (c) | $(\Theta-N+1)P(\Theta-N)$ | $o,o'$ | $r=\sqrt{(\Theta-N)/n}$ |
| $\{a,d\}\mid\{b,c\}$, part (b) | $(N+1)P(N)$ | $w,w'$ | $r'=\sqrt{N/n}$ |

**Reconstruction of the determinant argument.** At $X=1$, the zero block and the two block-triangular off-diagonal blocks leave the product of the two matching indicators, with a sign depending only on the class sizes. Exchanging the two halves of the symbols at $c,d$ relabels the matches. For the coefficient of $X^N$, put $R=N+1$. The truncated orthogonality identity (B.1) factors the off-diagonal blocks modulo $X^{2R}$, which preserves this coefficient. The auxiliary Schur determinant can be expanded by auxiliary rows. Each surviving term chooses equally many rows on its two sides, giving two subsets with total odd-weight energy $E\le N$. Each choice factors across $AD\mid BC$. There are at most $P(N)$ choices and at most $N+1$ ways to distribute the remaining coefficient degree. The determinant sign can be absorbed into an integer factor. [Truncation](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Identity.lean#L112) · [Block formula](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Identity.lean#L186) · [Expansion and counts](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Identity.lean#L455) · [Coefficient factorization](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Identity.lean#L569)

Reflecting $X\mapsto X^{-1}$ and exchanging $b,d$ gives $X^\Theta\Psi(X^{-1};a,b,c,d)=\Psi(X;a,d,c,b)$ before the final symbol relabeling. The powers from the four parties total $e_1+e_1+(e_2-2n_2)+(e_2+2n_2)=\Theta$, with $n_2$ the common size at $b,d$. This proves the degree bound and transfers the preceding factorization to complementary degree on $AB\mid CD$. The reflection takes place over the fraction field of $\mathbb Z[X]$ and returns a polynomial identity over $\mathbb Z$. Thus cancellation at a singular numerical value of $X$ never enters the argument. The summation identity and both integer decompositions then map into the given field $K$, even if some coefficients vanish there. The manuscript's working convention is $K=\mathbb R$ for these sections. The formal B.3 statements allow any field. ## One summand for both hub bounds

Fix $p,q\ge0$, the eight party labels, and first a spectral price $d_\varphi$. Place $(a,b,c,d)=(x,y,v,u)$. For $n\ge1$, let $E_1=\lfloor p^2 n\rfloor$ and $E_2=\lfloor q^2 n\rfloor$. Choose each class with at least $P(E_i)/(E_i+1)^2$ members. Its logarithmic cardinality is bounded below by $\log P(E_i)-2\log(E_i+1)$ and above by $\log P(E_i)$. Lemma B.1 gives a common $\kappa>0$ such that, after division by $s=\kappa\sqrt n$, the class logs tend to $p,q$. When a weight is zero, its energy-zero class contains only the empty symbol and its log is exactly zero. For positive weights the lower asymptotic in B.1 applies as $E_i\to\infty$. [Counting input, p. 22](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=22) · [B.1 declaration](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/States.lean#L136) · [Uniform class estimate](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Crossing.lean#L202)

Write $a_n=\log|\Sigma_1|$, $b_n=\log|\Sigma_2|$, $L_n=a_n d_{xv}+b_n d_{yu}$. Spectral multiplicativity and the wire exponent formula give $e^{L_n}$ for the product of the two wires. A cover by $\Theta+1$ terms gives $e^{L_n}\le\sum_j\varphi(\Psi_j)$, with the party push-forward understood. Thus at least one $N$ has $\varphi(\Psi_N)\ge e^{L_n}/(\Theta+1)>0$. This step permits cancellation in the original tensor entries. It uses nonnegativity of the spectral functional and its inequality for covers. [A.1–A.3, p. 19](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=19) · [Cover and choice of maximizer](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Crossing.lean#L97)

For that $N$, Lemma A.3 routes the two decompositions through their respective hubs. In each cut, the four outer wire capacities have logs $a_n,b_n,a_n,b_n$. The inner wire has the log of the corresponding product count from the table. Taking logarithms gives $L_n$ at most $\log(\Theta+1)$ plus the **minimum** of the two hub costs. This minimum records the common choice of tensor. Defining $r,r'$ from that index gives $r^2+(r')^2=\Theta/n\le2(p^2+q^2)$ exactly, at every scale. ## Error control in both passages

Put $\rho=2(p^2+q^2)$. Uniformly for every $0\le M\le\Theta\le\rho n$,

$$
\frac{\log((M+1)P(M))}{\kappa\sqrt n}
\le \sqrt{M/n}+\frac{\log(1+\rho n)}{\kappa\sqrt n}.
$$

The same last term bounds $\log(\Theta+1)/s$ and tends to zero for fixed $p,q$. This uniformity permits the maximizing $N$ to vary with $n$ and $\varphi$. It does not require the lower asymptotic in B.1 to hold at $N$ or at $\Theta-N$. Those indices may stay zero. Spectral distances are in $[0,1]$. For a target accuracy $\delta$, the formal proof takes each normalized error at most $\delta/8$, paying for the two demanded legs, four routed legs, one inner wire and one cover overhead. The shared witness lies in the closed quarter-disk $r,r'\ge0,\ r^2+(r')^2\le\rho$. Compactness and continuity of the minimum remove $\delta$ jointly. [Uniform product count](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Crossing.lean#L235) · [Spectral comparison](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Crossing.lean#L325) · [Compact maximum of the minimum](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Price/Cross.lean#L116)

For a general $K$-price, pull back to eight labels, allowing repeated points. Approximate the whole distance table to accuracy $\eta$ by a single nonnegative multiple $c\,d_\varphi$. Both comparison inequalities are homogeneous in the table. Replacing the table costs at most $(3(p+q)+\rho+1)\eta$ in either inequality since each hub weight is at most $\rho+1$. Compactness removes this second error, still with one pair. This includes $c=0$ and $p=q=0$ and involves no division by a hub distance. ## What Lemma C.2 consumes

Let $H=p d_{xv}+q d_{yu}$, let $A=p(d_{xo}+d_{vo'})+q(d_{yo}+d_{uo'})$, and let $B=p(d_{xw}+d_{vw'})+q(d_{uw}+d_{yw'})$. B.3 gives $H\le A+r d_{oo'}$ and $H\le B+r'd_{ww'}$. Add them, then use $r\lambda\le t_0r^2/2+\lambda^2/(2t_0)$ twice with the same $t_0>0$. The result is the numbered **display (B.2)**,

$$
2H\le A+B+t_0(p^2+q^2)+\frac{d_{oo'}^2+d_{ww'}^2}{2t_0}.
$$

At each translation of the eight vectors in a sheet of $W_n$, C.2 uses the local wire distances themselves as $p,q$. They may vary with the base point. With $t_0$ fixed, the left side consists of twice the sum of their squares. The pointwise growth assumption $W_n(P,P+g)\le K\|g\|_1$ bounds all terms for fixed displacements. Translation invariance identifies their squared means, and Cauchy–Schwarz bounds each mixed product by the product of its root mean squares. This gives the displayed form for the RMS length $\nu$ with its two wire lengths as weights. Apply it to the eight vectors scaled by $S$, divide by $S^2$, and use $\nu(Sg)/S\to N(g)$ for the finitely many displacements. The proof therefore needs no convergence of pointwise pairs $(r,r')$ under averaging. The remainder of C.2 uses a symmetric configuration to deduce the parallelogram law. ## Exact controls and scope

- **Coefficient and cut check.** For size one and energy four on both wires there are two symbols, $(\{0\},\{1\})$ and its reverse. An independent transcription of the manuscript's four-by-four determinant evaluated all 16 entries across all 17 degrees, with $\Theta=16$. The reflected coefficient identity, the two-wire indicator sum and both rank bounds held. On $(AD\mid BC,AB\mid CD)$ the endpoint ranks over $\mathbb Q$ are $(1,4)$ and $(4,1)$. At degree 8 both ranks are four over $\mathbb Q$ and both are zero after reduction modulo two. This checks a genuine cancellation and illustrates the need to select the summand after choosing the field and spectral point. The finite ranks alone do not certify the general integral product decompositions. [Reproducible check](../checks/pass11a_crossing.py)
- **Counterexample to a weaker claim.** In the Manhattan metric take $x=(-1,-1),v=(1,1),y=(-1,1),u=(1,-1)$ and hubs $o=(-1,0),o'=(1,0),w=(0,-1),w'=(0,1)$. At $p=q=1$, $H=8$, each outer cost is four, and both hub gaps are two. The first cut can use $(2,0)$ and the second $(0,2)$. Each pair fits the squared budget of four. To satisfy both cuts simultaneously requires $r,r'\ge2$, hence a squared budget of at least eight. At $t_0=1$ the scalar display would require $16\le14$. The example satisfies the ordinary metric axioms and shows exactly where price structure enters. [Exact arithmetic](../checks/pass11a_crossing.py)
