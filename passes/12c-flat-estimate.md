## E.7 · The shared selector and its disappearing cost

Fix a circuit instance and the $J\ge1$ balanced sets being enumerated. E.4 gives a sum of restricted hub tensors $t=\sum_j f_j(T_j)$. Multiplying $n$ copies of the sum yields one term per ordered word of length $n$. For each multiplicity vector $\tau$, choose a representative word. Every word of that type is a permutation of the representative because the sets of positions in each letter’s fiber have equal sizes. A diagonal tensor on the output parties shares the same word number at every retained party. It lets them all apply the corresponding permutation of positions. At the other parties the output has at most one index value, so choosing a different ordering there has no effect. The resulting single restriction gives exactly the sum of all the words in this type, including repeated or cancelling contributions. [Finite permutations and selector](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Price/BorderFew.lean#L31) · [Restriction and trivial outside legs](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Price/BorderFew.lean#L88)

The formal route embeds exact restrictions as degree $D=0$ in the coefficient formulation. Its general extraction factor $(nD+1)^{|V|}$ is therefore one here. With $M=\max_j\varphi(T_j)$, at most $(n+1)^J$ types and $J^n$ words per type, this gives
$$
 \varphi(t)^n\le (n+1)^J (J^{g_\varphi(P)} M)^n.
$$
For fixed $J$, taking roots removes the polynomial factor. The zero case $M=0$ also follows directly from the inequality at $n=1$. No division by a tensor value or by a type multiplicity is required, including in positive characteristic. E.7 returns an inequality with no residual $n$. E.10 applies it separately in each dimension. **Independent controls.** At tensor power two take the two two-party tensors $e_0\otimes e_0$ and $e_1\otimes e_1$. In type $(1,1)$, the words $01,10$ give a diagonal two-by-two matrix on these retained word indices. Separate word choices at the two parties give four ones. Also, with $J=n$, *the actual number of types* is at least $2^{n-1}$: choose the first $n-1$ counts independently in $\{0,1\}$ and give the last letter the remaining count. Its $n$th root cannot tend to one. These examples isolate two requirements of the argument. [Exact checks](../checks/pass12c_flat.py)

## E.8–E.9 · One tolerance, one base point, $k$ midpoint steps

For finite positions $V'$ and nonempty facet-centre set $P$, let $q=|P|$, $R=\sum_{u,v\in V'}\|u-v\|$, and $\eta>0$ be the permitted error per midpoint step. The formal E.8 proof permits
$$
 d=\min\left(1,\frac{\eta^2}{(q^2+1)(2R+4)}\right).
$$
If all scaled wire distances are within $d$ of their Euclidean distances, each Euclidean midpoint is within $\lambda/2+3d/2$ of its endpoints in the scaled metric, where $\lambda$ is their scaled separation. The $3d/2$ covers one endpoint error and half the opposite-edge error. The extension from A.7 provides a true midpoint in the enlarged price. C.1 bounds its squared distance to the old midpoint position by $\lambda(3d/2)+(3d/2)^2$. Since $\lambda\le R+1$ and $d\le1$, each distance is at most $\sqrt{d(2R+4)}$. Summing over $q$ positions costs at most $\eta$. This threshold precedes every choice of spectral point, scale and midpoint triple on $V'$. [Midpoint comparison](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L135) · [Sum of displacements](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L148) · [Explicit tolerance](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L192)

For a requested E.9 error $\varepsilon>0$, enlarge $V$ to $V'=V\cup\{-1/2,0,1/2\}^k$. Choose $d$ for $\eta=\varepsilon/(2k)$ on this enlarged set. Flatness supplies a *single* $(\varphi,c)$ with pair errors smaller than $\min(d,\varepsilon/2)$. The selected point, scale and $d$ can depend on $k,V',P,\varepsilon$. Their rates of growth or decay never enter circuit size. The manuscript allocates $\varepsilon/(k+1)$ to the base and each level. The formal proof allocates $\varepsilon/2$ to the base and divides the remaining half between the $k$ levels. Both totals are $\varepsilon$. [Manuscript, p. 36](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=36) · [Actual order of choices](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L318)

**Independent reconstruction of the maps.** Index a centre by a sign $s$ and coordinate $i$. With a uniform sign vector $\chi$, start with the diagonal position $s\chi_i\chi/2$. Average over the first $j$ signs with all later signs held fixed. For $i\le j$, the mixed expectations vanish and $\mathbb E\chi_i^2=1$, giving $s e_i/2$. For $i>j$, the first $j$ coordinates vanish and the rest are $s\chi_i\chi_x/2$. These are the manuscript’s maps. Averaging over one more sign gives their pointwise midpoint identity. This averaging is solely a formula for Euclidean positions. E.8 supplies the *inequality* for the spectral exponent.

Writing $h_j(\chi)=c\,g_\varphi(\alpha_{j,\chi})$, each initial map has image within the two ends of one diagonal, so $h_0\le\sqrt k+\varepsilon/2$. At level $j+1$,
$$
 h_{j+1}(\chi)\le \tfrac12 h_j(+,\chi)+\tfrac12 h_j(-,\chi)+\eta.
$$
Thus $h_j\le\sqrt k+\varepsilon/2+j\eta$ for every sign choice. The number of sign branches never multiplies the error because the two coefficients sum to one. At level $k$ the map is the identity on $P$. Pullback to $V$ preserves its image exponent. Independent enumeration checked 2,526 maps in dimensions one through six. Lean checked the same induction for arbitrary real losses depending on the level. ## E.10–E.11 · The paying cut and the target size

Let $N=k(k-1)/2$ and set the *outer* distance tolerance $\Delta=1/(1000k)$. The logical end parties of the $N$ wires can share geometric positions. The hyperedge exponent under placement depends only on the image of the end map. Thus charging the selector costs $c\,g_\varphi(P)\log_2 J\le k(\sqrt k+\Delta)$ once across all ends. E.10 chooses both hubs for **every** balanced subset, applies E.9 to the resulting one finite configuration with $\varepsilon=\Delta$, and pulls the result back to the labelled parties. Only then does E.7 supply a maximizing cut. Each candidate circuit may have different summands at different cuts. E.4 and the hub representation allow that freedom. [Hub sum](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Flat.lean#L345) · [Exponent of repeated positions](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Rate.lean#L118) · [Geometry before cut selection](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Rate.lean#L173) · [Cut selected](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Rate.lean#L218) · [E.7 caller](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Rate.lean#L299)

For each balanced cut, the signed hub direction pairs with its vector of coordinate signs to count $|S|(k-|S|)\ge2k^2/9$ crossing edges. Cauchy–Schwarz gives direction norm at least $2k^{3/2}/9$. Hubs at radius $2/(9\sqrt k)$ save at least $4k/81$ in total outer distance. With gate count $L\ge1$, the manuscript’s displayed **(E.2)** on p. 37 implies
$$
 \ell N(1-\Delta)\le k(\sqrt k+\Delta)
    +\ell(N-4k/81+k^2\Delta)
    +(4/(9\sqrt k)+\Delta)\log_2 L .
$$
The original-wire and two outer-leg errors cost at most $3\ell k^2\Delta/2$. It is therefore at most $0.0015\,\ell k$. The name of the cut costs at most $0.015\,\ell k$ for $\ell\ge100\sqrt k$. The positive remaining margin exceeds $\ell k/31$, and the coefficient of $\log_2 L$ is at most $0.446/\sqrt k$. This recovers the denominator 14 and hence the stated 16. In the formal version the exact counts $N$ and $2N$ yield an even smaller error. The theorem covers the printed variables with the same gate count: rename the occurring variables into the larger formal list, or send the unused variables to zero going back.
