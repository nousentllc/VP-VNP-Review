# What the five public Lean endpoints assert

**Verdict.** The exact statement models nonuniform, division-free arithmetic circuits with unrestricted field constants and fan-out. The VNP witness has polynomial length. The all-fields conclusion is strict inclusion. The border conclusion rules out polynomial-size approximations of a VNP family over each field. For this circuit model, allowing formal Laurent series and allowing rational functions regular only at the *output* give the same approximation predicate at each fixed size. The source proves the step needed in the less immediate direction. [Printed definitions, §§1.1 and 1.3](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=2) · [Public Lean declarations](../primary/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L20) · [Solution wrappers](../primary/vp-vnp/Solution.lean#L25)

## Circuit, family and quantifiers

1. **Circuits.** A natural-number-indexed list only constrains its first $s$ entries and requires the output among those entries. Each is a variable, an arbitrary scalar, or the sum or product of two earlier entries, which may coincide. For a smaller circuit, append scalar zero gates as needed. At $s=0$ no output index exists. A topological order realizes each finite fan-in-two circuit DAG as such a list. Conversely, choosing operands witnessing each step gives a DAG. Input and constant gates count; coefficients have no representation-length charge. Subtraction is available using $-1$ and multiplication. Equality is in a polynomial ring over $F$ with no degree restraint on intermediate gates, and reuse places no constraint on the number of outgoing edges. Over $\mathbb F_p$, $x^p-x$ evaluates to zero at every base-field element but differs from the zero polynomial, illustrating which lower-bound question is encoded. [Definition](../primary/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L23) · [Printed model](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=4)

2. **Which polynomial.** HC uses one successor permutation with a single cycle on the whole vertex set, counted once. A chosen starting vertex creates no extra factor. For the permanent the public definition gives $\prod_i X_{\sigma(i),i}$. Reindexing by $j=\sigma(i)$ and summing over $\sigma^{-1}$ yields the printed $\prod_j X_{j,\sigma(j)}$ literally as the same polynomial. HC is covered at every characteristic, including two. The permanent clause assumes $p\ne2$. The positive absolute $c$ precedes $p$, then $m_0(p)$ precedes the field and all matrix sizes and circuits. The declaration admits every natural $p$ syntactically. For a field, characteristic is zero or prime, so the other cases are vacuous. The real permanent specialization has its own endpoint. [Families and rate](../primary/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L29) · [Characteristic cases](../primary/vp-vnp/Solution.lean#L26) · [Real specialization](../primary/vp-vnp/Solution.lean#L50)

3. **VP and witnesses.** For a family indexed by $n$, one integer $a$ bounds its variable count, total degree and gate count by $n^a+a$ at every $n$. Ordinary polynomial bounds have this form after increasing the exponent. Choose $a$ large enough for $n^a+a$ to dominate any three fixed polynomial bounds for $n\ge2$, and enlarge it again to absorb the values at $0,1$. Each index can have its own circuit. VNP quantifies $r:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ and a witness polynomial $g_n$ with variables in the disjoint union $X_n\sqcup\mathrm{Fin}(r(n))$. Its family must be in VP. Taking cardinalities gives $r(n)\le |X_n|+r(n)\le n^a+a$, even when some declared variables are unused. Substitution at all Boolean witnesses and addition give the target as a formal polynomial in $X_n$. Degree cannot increase on substitution and addition; the target therefore inherits polynomially bounded degree and number of variables. The exponential number of Boolean assignments is intentional. [Public VP and VNP](../primary/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L75) · [Valiant's original formulation](https://doi.org/10.1145/800135.804419) · [Modern witness definition, §1](https://www.cse.iitk.ac.in/users/nitin/papers/PresentableVNP.pdf)

4. **Strictness.** For $r(n)=0$ there is exactly one function from the empty index type to Bool. Renaming the input variables preserves their circuit bound and evaluation, giving $\mathrm{VP}\subseteq\mathrm{VNP}$. The rooted order-table witness puts HC in VNP. There is one empty table with weight one at $n=0$ and one rooted table at $n=1$; the large-size lower bound concerns only $m\ge m_0$. If HC lay in VP, its bound $n^a+a$ would eventually be at most $n^{c(\log_2 n)^{1/3}}$, contradicting the strict lower bound. Thus each individual field has proper inclusion. ## Border approximation at a fixed size

The public predicate asks for $q$ over $F((\varepsilon))$ computed in at most $s$ gates. For each monomial $x^d$, the coefficients of $[\!x^d\!]q$ vanish at every negative power and its constant coefficient equals $[\!x^d\!]f$. Since both polynomials have finite monomial support, this is precisely $q=f+\varepsilon g$ with $g\in F[[\varepsilon]][x]$. Intermediate gates may have poles and positive powers of $\varepsilon$ can multiply monomials of high degree. VPBar bounds the degree of the target $f_n$. The Laurent condition tests every monomial of the output, including those absent from $f$. A pointwise identity on base-field inputs would give a different test. [Definition 1.3](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=2) · [Formal predicate](../primary/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L53) · [Family predicate](../primary/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L81)

Write $A_s(K)$ for the existence of such a degeneration with constants in $K$, output regular with residue $f$, and the same gate-count convention. Laurent expansion of a rational function at zero respects addition and multiplication. Thus

$$
 A_s(F[\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{-1}])\Longrightarrow A_s(F(\varepsilon))
 \Longrightarrow A_s(F((\varepsilon))).
$$

In the reverse direction take any witness circuit over the series field. All coefficients at all its finitely many gates have valuations bounded below by some $-B$. To preserve every coefficient through order zero, it suffices to approximate the output gate to order one. At a product the propagated error is $a\,\delta b+b\,\delta a+\delta a\,\delta b$. Precision $t+B$ in both operands gives precision $t$ at that gate for $t\ge0$. Addition takes the minimum of the operand precisions. Working backwards through finitely many gates gives a finite cutoff at each scalar gate, including a shared gate by retaining enough digits for every use. Truncating its series there gives a Laurent polynomial. The same $s$ gate positions now produce an output with the same negative and constant coefficients. A naive cutoff at order one at every gate would fail for the product $\varepsilon^{-1}\varepsilon$. [Pole bound](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Rate/Truncate.lean#L147) · [Induction on gates](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Rate/Truncate.lean#L184) · [Same-size witness and converse](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Rate/Truncate.lean#L241)

Consequently all three predicates agree for each $s$ under the common leaf-count convention. The rational-function formulation uses constants in $F(\varepsilon)$ and allows poles at intermediate gates; the output is coefficientwise regular at zero. The gate comparison requires each entire scalar, regardless of truncation length, to cost one constant gate. It gives no bound on bits needed to describe a truncation, on a prescribed approximation order in a different cost model, or on topological closures of rational points over arbitrary fields. [Bürgisser's rational-function definition, §6.1](https://www3.math.tu-berlin.de/algebra/work/mfcs2001-contrib.pdf) · [Publisher approximation argument, Appendix N](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=62)

Finally, exact computation is a constant degeneration. The two border endpoints assert the same *form* of quantitative gate lower bound for approximants and $\forall F,\ \mathrm{VNP}_F\not\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{VP}}_F$. The HC witness gives the required counterexample family at each field. The direction of this last assertion does not settle containment of $\overline{\mathrm{VP}}_F$ in VNP. The public exact and approximate rate predicates quantify their positive constants separately; the publisher's proofs exhibit the common choice $1/1600^2$.
