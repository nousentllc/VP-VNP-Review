# Pass 15 · How the diagonal earns its value guarantee

Edition 12 builds on the reconciled Edition 11 at `a6868a523e92ddf262de29af8bcba07580f6c0bb`. All manuscript and program references below concern the [pinned publisher manuscript, pp. 57–59](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=57). Write $H=16h$, $h\ge17$. The base ring $A$ supplies ordinary weights. Its commutative algebra $R$ supplies the factors requested by name. A bill is the ordered list of such requests on an accepting computation. The accelerator has two clauses with different domains. For every short input, clause (i) gives all-run halting, bounds the cost of each accepting run and limits its names. Clause (ii) equates weighted values only when the input itself is plain. Plainness requires halting of the encoded program on its encoded argument, an empty bill on **each** accepting run, and a time bound on those runs. [Definitions](../primary/vp-vnp/NS1/Accel.lean#L23-L60)

## K.4 · Postponement

Fix $J$ with $T<2^J$. Guess one $J$-bit counter per leaf of the trie. The wrapper decrements exactly the counter for each name that the simulated run requests. It accepts when every counter reaches zero and returns the ordinary weight with an empty bill. For a supported bill the initial vector is uniquely its histogram. Within a tally the ordered list of ordinary weights is preserved, including duplicate occurrences, zero and cancellation. A zero coefficient can erase the value of an entire nonempty group. Under the formal lookup hypothesis an unsupported request can have value zero and no matching vector. This explains why the printed leaf-membership assumption matters for the stronger claim about every original accepting run. [K.4 theorem](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Postpone.lean#L19-L58) · [Detailed derivation](../report/pass15-k4.tex)

With trie height η and node count Q′, the wrapper's accepting cost is at most (1000+100(η+J+1))T+40Q′(J+1)+40. Its encoded input has at most |WF|+|p|+|z|+4Q′(J+1)+3 nodes. Both estimates apply to a fixed tally. The number of possible tallies can be exponential. The model guesses a tally per computation and takes a weighted sum over computations.

## K.5 · One cut and all rounds

For a round lowering the accepting-cost exponent from f+8 to f, take the cut clock τ′=v^(f+9), the input allowance s=v^(α(H−32)+1), and the old accelerator at scale v. Shortness alone bounds each cut listing, its piece count and every piece's size. The listing costs at most v^(f−15), and a list has at most v^(f+11−H) pieces. For each piece allow v^(H−16)+30 operations. The leading product is v^(f−5). Adding the guess, listing and fixed overhead yields at most v^f per accepting computation. On the value side the order is different. The original input's plainness permits the pre-evaluator, K.4 supplies plainness of the postponed input, and the cut's value clause supplies plainness of every piece. Only then does each piece qualify for the old accelerator's value clause. [Implementation](../primary/vp-vnp/NS1/Scale.lean#L64-L120) · [Round bound](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Next.lean#L63-L150)

The new program quotes the previous program as data. Its code size starts at |UF|≤v and grows additively by at most v^(hα+2)+v^(H+1) per round. After 3α rounds the time exponent in base v is Hα+1−24α, which is at most α(H−17). At h=17 and α=5 the last exponent is 1241. The minimum needed to apply that round is 1240 and the promised endpoint exponent is 1275. With v′=v^α the final code fits (v′)^(4h), and the exponent allowance is B+51hα²lg v, for the same constant trie. The result gives the two operational clauses at v′. Reapplying the full size predicate requires its additional height and name bounds. [Round induction](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Next.lean#L270-L325) · [Detailed derivation](../report/pass15-k5.tex)

## K.6 · Self-application in dependency order

1. Lift the finitely many named constants into the subalgebra they generate over A. Reflect the accelerator along its injection into R, then push it forward to a maximal quotient. Finite type over the integers makes this quotient a finite field E. All syntax and resource bounds survive. The maps can collapse constants, while preserving the value equalities. [Reduction](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/NoAcc.lean#L20-L47)
2. For the whole finite field choose one index red(i) for the function x ↦ x^(2^i), uniformly over x. K.5 first uses α=max(5,a), then α=5 at successive scales. The trie remains fixed and the cubic logarithmic bound on its height and exponent allowance propagates. Unbounded scales allow choosing u *after* the finite table has been fixed. [Common power functions](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/NoAcc.lean#L49-L76) · [Scale invariant](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/NoAcc.lean#L222-L242) · [Choice of u, J and X](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/NoAcc.lean#L353-L388)
3. The self-application P(P) either accepts with weight 1 or guesses a tally e, names its scalar λ^e, and runs D on the description z_e of the postponed computation. The same bit address and trie occur both in the scalar names and the wrapper. Bound the syntax of P and z_e before evaluating them. Every |z_e|≤u^(H−32). [Code and shortness](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/NoAcc.lean#L103-L159)
4. Apply **only D.run** to those short descriptions. This evaluates P(P), yields halting on all branches and bounds the accepting cost by T=u^(H−15). Bill length is at most accepting cost and T<2^J. Every name agrees with lookup in the expanded trie. At this point the operational expansion for P(P) contains the actual values of D(z_e), which can be written without using a correctness guarantee. [Operational identity](../primary/vp-vnp/NS1/Diag.lean#L25-L33) · [Application and bill checks](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/NoAcc.lean#L160-L186)
5. Apply K.4 to those computations of P(P). For each e the wrapper halts and every accepting bill is empty. Its time estimate fits u·T=u^(H−14)≤u^H. Together these facts give Plain(θ,u^H,z_e). The time needed by D(z_e) and the time allowed for the program described by z_e belong to different computations. [Plainness proof](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/NoAcc.lean#L188-L209)
6. Now substitute **D.val**, once per e, using both the shortness proof and the plainness proof. K.4 gives V=Σ_e λ^e pv(z_e) with the canonical map from A to E understood. The operational formula gives V=1+Σ_e λ^e val(D(z_e)). The substitution makes the two sums equal. Subtracting V gives 1=0, impossible in E. K.7 applies this exclusion at precisely the scale and size that K.3 produced. [First value call and contradiction](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/NoAcc.lean#L210-L220) · [Scale match](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Scales.lean#L141) · [Detailed derivation](../report/pass15-k6.tex)

## Evidence and limits

The [review Lean file](../checks/pass15_plain.lean) isolates the use of K.4 in an accelerator substitution and gives an actual two-step program with a nonempty bill. The latter proves that all-run halting and a bound on accepting time can hold while plainness fails at every budget. The three [K.4](../checks/pass15_k4.py), [K.5](../checks/pass15_k5.py) and [K.6](../checks/pass15_k6.py) diagnostics exercise finite data and exact integer budgets. The hypothetical accelerator is used as a premise. The tests do not construct one. The [Pass 15 receipt](../checks/pass15_lean.json) is a fresh targeted build, separate from the [Edition 11 complete build](../checks/pass14_release.json) and [two-kernel Comparator record](../checks/pass14_comparator.json).

A review strengthening resolves the equality case of the K.4 clock. Its Lean proof adapts the publisher’s bill-length induction and reuses the K.4 wrapper and summation proof. Every actual accepting run pays at least one step beyond its number of name requests. Thus T≤2^J still fits every counter and gives all four K.4 conclusions with the same bounds. With 2^J nested name instructions around one quote, the unique accept costs 2^J+1 and requests one leaf 2^J times. A single root leaf of value 2 over the integers gives original value 2^(2^J) and a zero sum over J-bit wrappers. Hence the inclusive cutoff is sharp uniformly over programs.
