# Pass 11C · How J.2 splits a weighted computation

**November 25, 2026.** Fix the boundary states, routing data, valid packet counts and one bounded good outcome. At that point J.2's product formula follows from a weight-preserving bijection. Every current packet whose holders lie in different pieces gets one shared boundary value. The local piece assignments can then be summed independently. A separate tree argument bounds the amount of boundary data incident to each piece. Together these explain two steps that passes 4 and 6 took from J.2. The upstream law that transforms the original network into a sum of thin trees remains an input to this reconstruction. ## Exactly what gets identified

Let $U$ be a finite set of packet identifiers, $h_0,h_1:U\to P$ their holders, and $g:P\to G$ the partition of the nodes. A global assignment $y(p,u)$ is forced to the distinguished empty value off the seen packet/holder coordinates. For each **current** $u$, the two holders' copies agree. A seen packet consumed in an earlier move remains an input to the accumulated node tables; the generic final contraction imposes no current-wire equality on it. The actual tables can impose their own restrictions. Each node contributes its local weight $\phi_p(y_p)$ in the common commutative ring. [Source definitions](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Cut/Play.lean#L28) · [Seen, used and current](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/G/Defs.lean#L231) · [Final piece value](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/G/Defs.lean#L304)

**Independent derivation.** Set $B=\{u\in{\rm Cur}: g(h_0u)\ne g(h_1u)\}$. Given a valid $y$, define $b(u)=y(h_0u,u)$ on $B$ and set $b$ to empty off $B$. For each group $j$, restrict $y$ to nodes with $g(p)=j$, padding every other coordinate with the same empty value. Within a group the ordinary current-wire agreements persist. At a cut both local copies must equal the single specified $b(u)$.

Conversely, given a boundary word and valid local assignments $y_j$, take $y(p,u)=y_{g(p)}(p,u)$. Agreement on an inner current packet comes from its group. Agreement on a cut packet follows because both group restrictions refer to the same $b(u)$. These maps are inverse, including all padded coordinates. Each node enters one local product, so the bijection preserves its product of weights. Finite distributivity therefore gives

$$
 \sum_{y\ {\rm valid}}\prod_p\phi_p(y_p)
   =\sum_{b\ {\rm on}\ B}\ \prod_{j\in G}
     \left(\sum_{y_j\ {\rm valid\ at}\ b}\prod_{p:g(p)=j}\phi_p(y_{j,p})\right).
$$

The statement allows parallel packet IDs and self-loops. A self-loop stays in its one group and has one holder coordinate. An empty group contributes one, since it has one all-empty assignment and an empty product. A cut entry may itself carry the distinguished empty value in this algebraic identity. Pass 6 separately traced why the actual evaluator may omit such an entry when its active factor vanishes. The bijection here concerns assignments in one final network. The preceding law expansion can change the number of terms representing a weighted computation. [Cut set and local constraints](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Cut/Groups.lean#L25) · [Publisher's two inverse maps](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Cut/Groups.lean#L80) · [J.2 applies the group identity](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Pieces.lean#L86)

**Counterexample to losing packet identity.** Put two parallel current binary packets in two pieces, with a third, consumed packet between the same holders. The left factor is nonzero only when its consumed copy is 0 and the value of a local self-loop is 0. It assigns weights $2,-1$ to the live vectors $01,10$. The right factor requires its consumed copy to be 1 and assigns weights $3,5$ to those vectors. All other entries have weight zero. The direct contraction and grouped formula both give $2\cdot3-1\cdot5=1$. Identifying parallel packets by endpoints gives zero. Forcing the two consumed copies equal also gives zero. Guessing the two live vectors independently gives $(2-1)(3+5)=8$, which even changes the value in characteristic two. The exact enumeration includes an empty value distinct from the bit zero. These finite tables test the general regrouping lemma; they need not arise from a particular tower law. [Reproducible enumeration](../checks/pass11c_packets.py)

**Why a consumed packet can lose its equality.** At a single H.2 move let $O\subseteq{\rm Cur}$ be the consumed packets. For each outcome $r$, its new packet IDs are disjoint from everything already seen. The old weight $E(y)$ is unchanged on extending $y$ by new contents. On old assignments where $E(y)\ne0$, the move law represents the indicator of agreement on $O$ as a sum of new factors $C_r$ over outcomes and shared new contents. Multiply this identity by $E(y)$ and the agreement indicator for the untouched current packets. It then holds even when $E(y)=0$. Sum over $y$ and interchange the finite sums. Appending the new contents gives exactly the extended contraction with current set $({\rm Cur}\setminus O)\cup{\rm New}_r$ and weight $E\,C_r$. Thus the spent copies are still in the tables and enforcing their former equality as well would impose a second constraint. This derives the general substitution step conditional on the local tensor law; that local law's validity and the full induction producing H.2's thin tree remain distinct obligations. ## Where the savings come from

The bijection works for every partition. A partition meeting the time and space budgets requires more. Put $M=2K$, let $Y$ bound the total upward packet load, and call a nonroot node heavy when its subtree's given-node count divided by $4M$ plus its upward-packet count divided by $2Y$ exceeds $1/k$. The root is heavy by convention. At each nonroot stage, disjoint subtrees use at most $1/4+1/2$ of normalized weight, so fewer than $k$ nodes are heavy. Heaviness propagates to parents. A nonempty piece is either a heavy singleton or the full subtree of a light child of a heavy node. With root degree bounded by $C_0$, other child counts bounded by $b_0$, and depth $d$, the formal piece index is below $1+C_0+b_0dk$. The separate root term matters. Depth is polylogarithmic and the displayed index bound yields at most $k\Lambda$ pieces after absorbing constants. [Heavy count and monotonicity](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Heavy.lean#L143) · [Piece shapes](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Heavy.lean#L295) · [Piece count](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Heavy.lean#L363)

**Independent boundary argument.** All parent-child edges crossing the boundary of one nonempty piece touch a single node $x$. For a heavy singleton, $x$ is that node. For a light subtree the sole possible tree crossing is its root's parent edge. H.2 supplies the additional fact that every current, nondead packet between distinct nodes runs along a parent-child edge. All nondead boundary IDs incident to this piece can therefore be charged to the *same* node's H.2 incident-load bound, even if many packet IDs cross one edge. A connected subset alone would be insufficient: on the four-node path, the piece consisting of the middle two vertices has two disjoint boundary edges. The exceptional set of dead packet IDs is charged separately by its total size. “Dead” here denotes H.2's exceptional set and “consumed” above denotes removal from the current set by a move. The two play different roles. The proof yields

$$
  \#\{\text{current cut IDs incident to piece }j\}
  \ \le\ \#\{\text{nondead inter-node IDs at }x\}+\#\{\text{dead IDs}\}.
$$

After multiplying by the packet bit width and using H.2's load bound and J.2's choice of parameters, the per-piece cut budget is $KT\Lambda/v^{300}$. For its total-bit budget, the proof also charges the copies of every seen packet at its holders, the padded move slots, and the original given-node work; it bounds their combined bit count by $KT\Lambda/k$. The boundary estimate alone would not establish the total-bit estimate. The count of given blocks and parts is at most $CK/k$. These bounds apply to valid counts and good outcomes, as required by J.3. ## What J.3 and the circuit branch consume

J.2 assumes the stated variable-length laws over a commutative ring, $h\ge3$, and the gain inequality $1+8C_1\le h/4$. The powers $a,e$ are chosen before the finite automaton, whereas $C,v_0$ can depend on it. For sufficiently large $v$, one $K$ is chosen before $T,k$. The formal theorem allows $T\ge v^a,\ 2\le k\le v^{64}$, without the prose's upper bound on $\lg T$ at this particular step. Its bounded straight-line program may depend on $v,T,k$, takes packet counts and outcomes as inputs, and specifies the partition and node operations. J.3 separately accounts for encoding that program and running the evaluator. [J.2 statement and quantifiers](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Pieces.lean#L640) · [Partition computation](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Pieces.lean#L444) · [J.3 one-cut statement](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Cuts.lean#L95)

I.2 first gives the time-$KT$ automaton contraction over boundary states, valid rows and packet counts. Its local lemma bounds the incident packet count at any node with a nonzero initial table entry. Thus if a count vector exceeds the load bound at a node, each global summand contains a zero table entry there. This justifies filtering the vector before applying H.2 and regrouping. For surviving vectors, H.2 supplies the finite sum of final networks indexed by bounded good outcomes. This needs no converse from goodness to physical attainability, nor a bound on every abstract unbounded good tuple. J.2 reassembles the products in each such term by the bijection above. J.3 substitutes the resulting equality on **every short encoded input**, changing only the explicit node-load predicate to the equivalent straight-line test. Plainness is needed at the separate interface identifying the time-bounded universal automaton value with the original program value.
