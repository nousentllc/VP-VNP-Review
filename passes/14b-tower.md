## Cubic word diameter in the printed proof

H.1(d) on p. 44 states a bound of order (n+1)^7. The printed proof gives a cubic strengthening. The following is a human reconstruction for the same generators and every n≥0. Here h≥3 is fixed, R=F₂[s]/(s^(n+1)), ζ=1+s, and eᵢⱼ(u)=I+uEᵢⱼ. Each generator squares to the identity, so the word for an inverse has the same length.

1. **Initial words.** For distinct i,m,j the identity [eᵢₘ(u),eₘⱼ(v)]=eᵢⱼ(uv) holds with commutator xyx⁻¹y⁻¹. A simple forward path on the h-cycle produces coefficient one for i<j and coefficient ζ for i>j, since only the wrap has weight ζ. To create an upper ζ for 0<i<j, use middle index i−1 and coefficients ζ,1. For i=0<j<h−1 use middle index j+1 and coefficients 1,ζ. For the remaining corner (0,h−1) use index 1 and coefficients ζ,1. The preceding case has already supplied e₀₁(ζ). This is where h≥3 matters. The words for all ζ seeds are bounded by a common C_h depending only on h.
2. **Balanced multiplication.** For a≥2 choose a third index and commute words for ζ^⌊a/2⌋ and ζ^⌈a/2⌉. One level costs at most four times the larger word bound, giving length ≤C_h 4^⌈log₂ a⌉≤4C_h a². Multiplying powers consecutively would lose this estimate.
3. **Arbitrary coefficients.** The coefficients of (1+s)^j in increasing powers of s form a triangular basis-change matrix with a unit diagonal for j=0,…,n. Multiplication by the unit ζ shifts this basis to ζ,…,ζ^(n+1). Add its selected terms using eᵢⱼ(x+y)=eᵢⱼ(x)eᵢⱼ(y). The word length is at most 4C_h∑ₐ₌₁ⁿ⁺¹a²=O_h((n+1)³), including n=0.
4. **Reduction inside SL.** If every entry in a pivot column of an invertible remaining block lay in the unique maximal ideal (s), so would its determinant. A unit pivot always exists. A row swap costs three transvections in characteristic two. Upper and lower clearing together require at most h(h−1) additions of a multiple of one row to another. A determinant-one diagonal (u₀,…,u_{h−1}) is the product over i<h−1 of diagonals (uᵢ,uᵢ⁻¹) on coordinates i,h−1. On a coordinate pair, w(u)=e₁₂(u)e₂₁(−u⁻¹)e₁₂(u) has off-diagonal entries u and −u⁻¹, so w(u)w(−1)=diag(u,u⁻¹). Each diagonal pair costs six transvections. Including at most h−1 pivot exchanges gives a total of at most (h−1)(h+9) transvections. Reversing the elimination word proves the stated cubic estimate.

The finite H.1 routing check above has n=1 and cannot distinguish growth exponents. The pinned [Proposition H.1](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Paper/Tower.lean#L25) certifies the seventh-power statement. The cubic reconstruction supplies a stronger word-length bound to the same initial-routing interface without changing any lower-bound or separation claim.
