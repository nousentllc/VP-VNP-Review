# Pass 7 · Why the two cases cover every field

**November 24, 2026.** I reconstructed Theorem D.2 from the pinned manuscript and compared its eight steps with the formal source. Conditional on the paper’s price geometry, the argument produces a single price realizing all pairwise distances of the requested finite Euclidean configuration whenever contraction fails. The critical step is the move from equality of averages to equality in every admissible quadrangle. An exact metric example below shows what would fail if one skipped it. [Manuscript, pp. 30–31](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=30) · [Pinned source manifest](../source-lock.json)

## Statement, scope and quantifiers

Fix the circuit coefficient field K. A price on a finite set is a distance table in the closure of the nonnegative scalar multiples of spectral-point tables for K. Approximation is simultaneous in all entries. Prices may assign zero distance to distinct labels. Membership in this closure is the conclusion used here. [Price definition](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Price/Basic.lean#L26) · [Pullback definition on arbitrary sets](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Price/Basic.lean#L73)

For M = Mat_μ(𝔽₂), put mᵣ = D(r)², the mean of w(a,a+W)² over all base points a and all rank-r increments W. The auxiliary matrices are always binary. Distances and the averages are real, whatever the characteristic of K.

- **Contracting.** There exist μ ≥ 2 and a real s with 1 ≤ s < √μ such that D(μ) ≤ sD(1) for every K-price on M.
- **Flat.** For every finite-dimensional Euclidean space and every finite configuration in it, the full Euclidean distance table is a K-price.
- **D.2.** For each K at least one hypothesis holds. The proof proceeds by assuming failure of contraction. The choice of μ and ambient price space may vary with the requested Euclidean dimension. The conclusion gives neither an effective field classifier nor exclusivity. [Definitions D.1 and D.2](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L41) · [Conclusion](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L921)

## Steps 1–3 · Extracting a simultaneous extremum

1. Fix μ ≥ 3. For matrices U,V whose column spaces and row spaces are independently in direct sum, apply the CAT(0) quadrangle inequality to a, a+U, a+U+V, a+V. The defect is the sum of the four squared sides minus the sum of the two squared diagonals. It is nonnegative. Under uniform left and right invertible changes of basis and uniform a, the differences U,V,U+V are separately uniform within their respective rank classes. Both diagonals have difference U+V in characteristic two. Thus the average defect is 2(mᵢ+mⱼ−mᵢ₊ⱼ), and mᵣ ≤ r m₁ follows by induction. The rank classes in this argument have r ≤ μ. [Frame averages](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L127) · [Quadrangle and subadditivity](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L195)
2. Failure of contraction gives, for each s approaching √μ from below, a possibly different price with D(μ) > sD(1). The preceding bound excludes D(1)=0 for any such witness. Normalize by D(1). If N is the number of ordered rank-one steps across all base points, each normalized rank-one distance is at most √N. Any difference decomposes into at most μ rank-one increments, so the triangle inequality bounds every entry by μ√N. The normalized arrays lie in a closed, bounded subset of a finite-dimensional space. For example take sₙ=√(μ−1/(n+1)). A single convergent subsequence controls every pair. Continuity, the lower bound sₙ² and the upper bound μ give m₁=1, mμ=μ at its limit. Subadditivity gives μ ≤ mᵣ+mμ₋ᵣ ≤ r+(μ−r), so mᵣ=r for every r. [Coordinate bound](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L251) · [Closedness and compactness](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L79) · [Extremal price](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L306)
3. For each admissible rank pair the mean defect is now zero. Each summand is nonnegative, and the finite frame enumeration reaches every transversal pair and every base point. Therefore every such quadrangle has zero defect. The equality clause of C.4(b) gives equality of opposite sides. Writing nₐ(U)=w(a,a+U)², one obtains nₐ₊ᵥ(U)=nₐ(U) and w(a+U,a+V)²=2nₐ(U)+2nₐ(V)−nₐ(U+V). These are the pointwise identities used in the next steps. [Equality on every transversal pair](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L366) · [Opposite sides and diagonals](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L399)

## Steps 4–7 · From quadrangles to the rank of each difference

Put Bₐ(U,V) = (nₐ(U+V)−nₐ(U)−nₐ(V))/2. Applying the preceding identities at a+V and at a cancels the third difference of nₐ for a **jointly transversal triple**. Thus Bₐ(U,V+W)=Bₐ(U,V)+Bₐ(U,W) on exactly the triples the exchange argument uses. Joint transversality requires direct sums for all three row spaces and all three column spaces. [Triple hypothesis](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L86) · [Cancellation](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L420)

For a fixed rank-one U, write χ(u,x)=Bₐ(U,uxᵀ). The binary identity u₁x₁ᵀ+u₂x₂ᵀ=u₁(x₁+x₂)ᵀ+(u₁+u₂)x₂ᵀ gives a difference of two χ-values equal to an expression independent of x₁. Replacing x₁ by x₁+x₂ reverses the sign of the difference and preserves the hypotheses. The difference is therefore zero over the reals. These moves connect all permitted row vectors and likewise all permitted column vectors. In the quotient by the row space of U, different nonzero cosets connect in one move, and two representatives of the same coset can pass through a second nonzero coset. This requires codimension at least two. Symmetry and a third rank-one direction transversal to either of two specified rank-one directions give one common value λ(a) on all transversal rank-one pairs. This explains the choice μ ≥ 3. [Exchange and allowable moves](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L485) · [Connectivity and common value](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L527)

**Independent derivation of Step 6.** The quadrangle identities give Bₐ₊ᵥ(V,W)=−Bₐ(V,W) for a transversal pair. For every rank-one V one can choose a transversal rank-one W, so λ(a+V)=−λ(a). Take V=E₁₁ and U=E₁₂. Each of V, U and V+U has rank one. Reaching a+V+U by two moves preserves λ(a), while the direct move reverses it. Thus λ(a)=0. The two moves need no mutual transversality because each has its own comparison direction. The manuscript instead reflects along two independent diagonal increments and along their rank-two sum, using a third direction. Both arguments use real-valued λ. [Publisher’s reflection proof](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L578) · [Focused scalar lemmas](../checks/pass07_reflection.lean)

Decompose transversal matrices in common row and column bases. Additivity and λ=0 eliminate the cross terms, so nₐ is additive on transversal sums. Applying the exchange again with fixed matrix zero gives a common nₐ(V)=c(a) on rank-one V. Symmetry gives c(a+V)=nₐ₊ᵥ(V)=nₐ(V)=c(a). Rank-one moves generate every matrix difference, hence c is constant. Its mean m₁ is one. A rank-r difference decomposes as a transversal sum of r rank-one directions, giving **w(a,b)²=rank(a−b) for every pair in the same price**. [Transversal decomposition](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L625) · [Rank-one normalization and rank formula](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L654)

## Step 8 · Filling one cube

Proposition C.3 supplies an ambient complete CAT(0) space whose distance table is itself a K-price. The extremal price pulls back from it. On binary diagonal matrices, rank of a difference is Hamming distance, precisely the squared Euclidean distance between the corresponding cube vertices. To add coordinate c, fix two partially filled points and let E be the sum of squared differences in the other coordinates. For their two filling segments, the previous induction stage supplies all six squared distances of the endpoints. The two verticals are 1, the two horizontals are E, and both diagonals are E+1. C.4(f) then forces the squared distance at fractions s,t to be E+(s−t)², including E=0. This constructs one map preserving all pairs on the solid cube. Knowing only the edge lengths would leave the diagonals undetermined. [Formal fill](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Alternative.lean#L736) · [Flat rectangle identity](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Toolkit.lean#L440) · [Ambient price realization](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Curvature.lean#L211)

For any finite configuration in ℝᵏ, choose μ=k+3, put its coordinates inside the cube by a translation and common positive rescaling, and pad with zeros. The formal choice R=1+Σ|xᵢ| is positive even for empty or coincident configurations. Pull back the ambient price and multiply by 2R to recover the original distances. For the exact all-fields theorem the caller chooses a comparison field for each characteristic, invokes D.2 on it, then uses a separate circuit-transfer lemma to treat other fields of that characteristic. D.2 covers the field cases. The work of covering every circuit remains in the two branch arguments and the transfer. ## Counterexamples to stronger readings

| Stronger inference | Independent exact check |
|---|---|
| The rank averages alone fix each distance. | On Mat₃(𝔽₂), start with squared distances rank(a−b). Change the squared distances of the unordered pairs {0,E₁₁} and {0,E₃₃} to 7/8 and 9/8. Symmetry and all rank-class averages survive. The square roots give a metric because every nonzero side is at least √(7/8) and the maximum is √3 < 2√(7/8). The square with directions E₁₁,E₂₂ at zero has defect 31/8−4=−1/8. This metric fails the price quadrangle condition, which is exactly what Step 3 needs. |
| Pairwise transversality suffices for Step 4. | E₁₁, E₂₂ and (e₁+e₂)(e₁+e₂)ᵀ are pairwise transversal. Their triple sum has rank two. Using n=rank gives B(U,V+W)=−1/2 while B(U,V)+B(U,W)=0. The jointly transversal statement survives. |
| The full matrix metric in Step 7 must itself be Euclidean. | On the sixteen binary 2×2 matrices inside a corner, let cₐ=(−1)^trace(a), with sum zero. A Euclidean squared-distance table would give Σcₐcᵦd(a,b)²=−2‖Σcₐxₐ‖² ≤ 0. For d²=rank(a−b) direct enumeration gives **+16**. The diagonal cube is enough for Step 8. This witness also checks the manuscript’s D.3 remark. |
