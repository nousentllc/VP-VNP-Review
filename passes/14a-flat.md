## 1. Which circuit and which tensor?

Fix a field K satisfying Flat, integers k≥2 and ℓ≥100√k and an arbitrary circuit of L gates for the printed polynomial f(k,ℓ). Its gates are variables, field constants or sums/products of earlier entries. The list may reuse entries, have zero coefficients and contain cancellation. Let E be the N=k(k−1)/2 edges a<b. Choose an ℓ-bit string on each edge. At each vertex use its incident strings as the index of its block variable. Sum the product of the k selected variables over all string assignments. A coefficient with one variable per block is one exactly when both endpoint copies of each string agree. Thus its coefficient array is a product of N equality wires of dimension 2^ℓ. The larger implementation variable set and the exact printed set admit circuits at the same gate bound. [Original circuit definition](../primary/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L23) · [Printed variable transfer](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Flat.lean#L143) · [Coefficient tensor](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Flat.lean#L286)

For A with k≤3|A|≤2k, E.4 collects one complementary cofactor per original gate in the exact multilinear component. A.3 uses one shared gate index over the two hubs of A. Hence the target tensor t is a sum of restrictions of J hub tensors T_A, where J is the number of balanced subsets. It lies between one and 2^k. T_A has a wire of dimension 2^ℓ from each end to its corresponding hub and one wire of dimension L between the two hubs. A restriction is a product of local linear maps, one per party. The output t has a separate logical party for each edge end, and subsingleton legs at all other parties. Distinct logical end labels may share a physical position later. ## 2. E.7 · Pay for one common selector

Let P be the end set. Write Φ for a spectral point and g(P) for the log base two of the value of the diagonal size-two tensor supported on P. A diagonal selector of size r has spectral value r^g(P). For a tensor power n expand t^⊗n by words w∈[J]^n. Fix a multiplicity vector τ, a representative word, and for each word of that type a permutation of positions taking the representative to it. One shared index among the rτ words enables each retained end to apply the same permutation to its n output positions. At a hub there is no nontrivial output index to permute. Thus the whole contribution of type τ is a single restriction of the representative product times the size-rτ selector. This reconstructs the *sum* of products of coefficients, including their multiplicities in the field.

Restriction monotonicity, the spectral bound for an ordinary tensor sum and multiplicativity now give

$$
 \Phi(t)^n\le(n+1)^J\big[J^{g(P)}\max_A\Phi(T_A)\big]^n.
$$

The exponent g(P) is nonnegative. We used rτ≤Jⁿ and bounded the number of types by (n+1)^J. The formal coefficient route has an additional factor (nD+1)^|V| at extraction degree D. Exact local maps give D=0. Fix k,J,Φ and all the tensors while taking n→∞. If the right-hand exponential base is zero the finite inequality already forces Φ(t)=0. Otherwise taking roots removes the polynomial overhead. The resulting inequality holds for each Φ. It permits a different paying cut A for each Φ. **Adversarial controls.** Flattening rank is a spectral point. For the ordinary two-party matrix E₀₀+E₁₁, the output rank is two and each input rank is one. If the selector were charged only on the singleton row party, g(P)=0 and the asserted right side would be one. The nontrivial column output violates precisely the omitted support hypothesis. In a different example use an equality wire on ab with c fixed, and one on ac with b fixed. Split off b or c, giving the rows (2,1) and (1,2) for their ranks. On this pair of test functionals the minimum of row maxima is two and the maximum of column minima is one. It diagnoses the error of allowing a different favorable functional for each term. The 12C diagonal control separately detects inconsistent word choices between two retained parties. ## 3. E.8–E.9 · Simultaneous distances and the output selector price

On a finite point configuration V′, fix maps a,b,m from q labels with mᵢ the Euclidean midpoint of aᵢ and bᵢ. Using one shared fair choice between maps a and b, A.7 (via its extension form) supplies an enlarged spectral point with a new label i′ for each i. Retain the product of all old two-dimensional wires and the diagonal tensor at m. The positive factors each have an old-site upper bound. Equality of the product restores the total wire product and old selector separately. Taking logs of the old wire product gives a sum of nonnegative deficits equal to zero, so all old distances are retained simultaneously. The two distances from i′ to aᵢ,bᵢ are each at most half their separation, giving equality by the triangle inequality. The new selector exponent is bounded by the mean of the two old placement exponents. [Witness preserving both old data](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L42) · [Midpoint and new exponent](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L109)

Write d for the wire-distance approximation tolerance, c≥0 for the scale, Dᵢ for the scaled separation of aᵢ,bᵢ, and R for the sum of Euclidean distances of all ordered pairs in V′. If d≤1, then Dᵢ≤R+1. Each distance from the old Euclidean midpoint mᵢ to aᵢ or bᵢ is at most Dᵢ/2+3d/2 in the scaled metric. The C.1 midpoint inequality gives distance from mᵢ to i′ at most √(Dᵢ·3d/2+(3d/2)²). Moving the q legs of the diagonal tensor one at a time changes the scaled exponent by at most the sum of these distances. This is bounded by q√(d(2R+4)). Choose d=min(1,η²/[(q²+1)(2R+4)]) for desired per-level error η. The E.8 tolerance precedes the spectral witness and every triple of placements on V′. Internal spectral extensions may vary with the triple. The inequality they prove is always for the original Φ. [Squared displacement](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L135) · [Move all legs](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L148) · [Tolerance and quantifiers](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L192)

For E.9 let P₀ be the set of actual end positions, a nonempty subset of the facet centres ±eᵢ/2. Add all half-cube ternary points to V to form V′. Given an outer tolerance Δ choose the previous d with η=Δ/(2k) and q=|P₀|. Flatness supplies **one** Φ and scale c approximating the Euclidean distances on V′ to min(d,Δ/2). For an end seᵢ/2 the initial map sends it to sχᵢχ/2 for a sign vector χ. For each χ the whole image has at most two positions on a diagonal of length √k, giving scaled exponent at most √k+Δ/2. At level j average over the first j signs holding the tail fixed. Each next map is the pointwise midpoint of two preceding maps, and each coordinate stays in the ternary half cube. Averaging E.8's two preceding bounds and adding η costs at most η per level. After k steps E[χᵢχⱼ]=1 if i=j and zero otherwise, so the end map becomes the identity. The final selector cost is at most √k+Δ in this same witness. Pull it back to all logical end and hub labels by the placement map. The image exponent of the end selector is unchanged at coincident sites. [Cube induction](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L318) · [Exponent of the image](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Rate.lean#L118)

## 4. E.10 · Geometric savings and the exact scalar budget

For each edge a<b, place its ends at +eₐ/2 and −eₐ/2. Given a balanced cut A, put sₐ=+1 on A and −1 outside. The direction u_A is the sum of sₐ eₐ for crossing edges a<b. Its dot product with s equals |A|(k−|A|)≥2k²/9, hence ‖u_A‖≥2k√k/9. Set γ=2/(9√k), x_A=γu_A/‖u_A‖ and use hubs ±x_A. The tangent bound ‖e/2−x‖≤1/2+‖x‖²−⟨e,x⟩ for unit e gives a sum of end-to-hub lengths at most N+2Nγ²−2γ‖u_A‖≤N−4k/81, because 2Nγ²=4(k−1)/81. The middle length is 2γ. Choose these hubs for *all* balanced sets before constructing V, V′ and the E.9 witness. Only after pulling that witness to the logical parties apply E.7 and take a maximizing A. Let S₁ be the sum of original wire exponents, S₂ the sum over the 2N outer wires at the selected cut, S₃ the middle wire exponent, and G the logical end selector exponent. Equality of the original and routed coefficient values is used through the sum-of-restrictions bound. The price of a wire of dimension h is h^d, with d its exponent. Taking base-two logs gives ℓ S₁≤kG+ℓ S₂+S₃ log₂L. The four estimates at Δ=1/(1000k) are cS₁≥N(1−Δ), cS₂≤N−4k/81+2NΔ, cS₃≤4/(9√k)+Δ, and cG≤√k+Δ. In particular the demand-and-supply wire error is 3ℓNΔ. Neither the number of cuts nor the number of sign branches multiplies that error. ## 5. E.11 and M.3 · The flat circuit contradiction

Let a circuit of size s compute a grid-complete target at m, with projection parameter κ. For HC, κ=40 over every field. For PERM, κ=3 and 2≠0. An affine substitution gives an f(k,ℓ) circuit of at most 5s gates whenever κk³ℓ2^(kℓ)≤m. For m≥2^[200(κ+3)³], take B=⌊log₂m⌋ and maximal r with 200r³≤B. Then r≥κ+2, and B+1≤200(r+1)³≤1600r³. Set k=r² and ℓ=100r. The fit bound follows from κr⁶(100r)≤2^(107r)≤2^(100r³). The original /16 estimate gives log₂(5s)≥25r⁴/4 and hence log₂s>r⁴≥(log₂m)^(4/3)/1600². This holds for each sufficiently large m, rather than only at the selected cubes. It contradicts any candidate at or below that size over a flat comparison field. The other disjunct of D.2, its quantitative circuit contradiction and M.1 transfer still enter the all-fields statement. It computes integer/rational results for 84 signed tensor-power entries, 1,428 balanced hub directions in k=2,…,10, two rank controls and the exact two scalar margins.
```sh
python3 checks/pass14a_margin_replay.py --mathlib "$MATHLIB_DIR" --output /tmp/flat14-margin.json
```
