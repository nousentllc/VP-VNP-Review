# Pass 11D · Field constants and the scope of the final bound

**Question.** How does a lower bound proved over one field constrain every circuit over every field of that characteristic, even when the circuit uses constants chosen separately for that circuit? Pass 1 located M.1–M.4. This pass reconstructs the same-size descent in M.1 and the order of choices in M.3. [Manuscript, pp. 60–62](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=60)

## Descent of one circuit

Fix a characteristic $p$ (zero or prime), an algebraically closed field $K$ of that characteristic, an arbitrary field $F$ of the same characteristic, and a polynomial $q$ with integer coefficients. If a list with $s$ gates computes the image of $q$ in $F[X]$, M.1 supplies a list with $s$ gates computing its image in $K[X]$. The definition permits the output to occur at any index below $s$. Multiplication, addition, constants and variables are the permitted gates. [Circuit predicate](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/LowDegree.lean#L25) · [M.1](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Transfer.lean#L21)

1. **Put the two fields in a common ring.** The manuscript uses an algebraic closure containing copies of the two fields, with $K$ the algebraic closure of the prime field. The formal proof handles any algebraically closed target of the same characteristic. Take $T=F\otimes_k K$ over the prime field $k$. This is nonzero, and the canonical maps from the fields to $T$ preserve their units. A common integral domain is unnecessary for the subsequent specialization. [Common ring](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Rate/Transfer.lean#L27)
2. **Retain the relations among all constants together.** Map the gate list into $T[X]$. Collect into one finite set the constant coefficients of its $s$ entries, which includes the value of every actual constant gate. For the polynomial ring over $K$ with a variable for each collected element, evaluate those variables at their elements of $T$. The kernel is proper. A maximal ideal containing the kernel gives a $K$-point by the weak Nullstellensatz. Evaluation at this point factors through the image subring $A$ of the first evaluation. The resulting map $A\to K$ preserves every polynomial relation among the collected elements. A homomorphism on the whole of $F$ is unnecessary. [Finite specialization](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Rate/Transfer.lean#L44) · [Gate constants actually collected](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Transfer.lean#L24)
3. **Preserve the computation and its length.** Lift each gate to $A[X]$ using its chosen gate witnesses; each sum or product can refer to the same earlier indices. Inclusion $A\hookrightarrow T$ is injective, so the lifted output is exactly the integer target mapped into $A[X]$. Mapping the list along $A\to K$ preserves the gate equations and the output. This needs a relation-preserving choice of constants once for that circuit. The existence claim allows different circuits to use different specializations. [Lifting steps](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Rate/Truncate.lean#L48) · [Mapping circuits](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Final/CClasses.lean#L74) · [Output identity](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Transfer.lean#L29)

**Counterexample to specializing directly into the prime field.** An element $a\in\mathbb F_4$ outside $\mathbb F_2$ satisfies $a^2+a+1=0$. Neither possible element of $\mathbb F_2$ satisfies the equation. A map preserving this relation cannot take its value there. The algebraic closure contains such a root. This obstructs this gatewise specialization. The existence of a different circuit for the output polynomial over the prime field is a separate question. [Focused obstruction and M.1 queries](../checks/pass11d_transfer.lean)

## An absolute exponent with a characteristic-dependent threshold

For each $p$ the proof chooses $K=\overline{\mathbb Q}$ or $\overline{\mathbb F_p}$, then invokes the field alternative over that one $K$. The numerical coefficient $c=1/1600^2$ is already fixed in the formal M.3. The flat bound has this coefficient; the contracting exponent has the form $a_p L^2/\log_2 L$, where $L=\log_2 m$ and $a_p>0$. The ratio to $cL^{4/3}$ is $(a_p/c)L^{2/3}/\log_2 L$, which diverges for each fixed $a_p>0$. For example, for any fixed $m>2$ one could make this ratio less than one by allowing $a_p$ to be sufficiently small. The limiting comparison alone supplies no threshold uniform in $p$. The paper explicitly leaves the cutoff ineffective. [Formal rate comparison](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Fields.lean#L64) · [Remarks on effectivity, p. 60](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=60)

M.3 obtains separate size cutoffs for HC and for the permanent on $K$, using a vacuous permanent clause at $p=2$, and takes their sum plus two. Only then does it quantify over $F$ and its circuits. HC and the permanent are integer polynomials that commute with coefficient maps. Consequently any violating circuit on $F$ would give a circuit of the same size violating the appropriate bound on $K$. HC uses M.2’s grid factor 40 in all characteristics. For the permanent the relevant reduction requires $2\ne0$. HC membership in VNP is a further input to M.4. [M.3 and its callers](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Fields.lean#L83) · [HC projection](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/HcGrid.lean#L18) · [Membership](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Fields.lean#L131)
