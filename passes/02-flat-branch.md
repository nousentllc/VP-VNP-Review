# Pass 2 · How a single estimate covers many cuts

**November 24, 2026.** Question from pass 1: can a small circuit evade the flat argument by using different balanced cuts in different summands? The argument explicitly allows that freedom. Three steps carry the quantitative bound.

### The decomposition and its quantifiers

**Source.** The input is an arbitrary circuit of size L for the complete-graph polynomial. At a product gate the component indexed by a set A of blocks is the sum over disjoint splittings B ⊔ C = A of the components at the two input gates. Once a balanced subset is reached, the proof collects terms by (gate, subset). For each balanced S, the output component T_S is a sum of at most L simple products across S and its complement. The total tensor is the sum of these T_S. See E.3–E.4, p. 33. The formal version works over commutative semirings. [Gate-level statement](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Flat.lean#L233)

**Independent counterexample to a possible shortcut.** With three blocks of variables a, b, c, consider a₁ Σᵢ bᵢcᵢ + b₁ Σᵢ aᵢcᵢ, with q ≥ 3 indices in each block. Each summand factors across one balanced cut. Flatten the sum with a as the row index and the pair (b,c) as the column index. Columns (2,2),(1,2),…,(1,q) give an identity minor of order q, so even two such summands can have rank q across the first cut. This rules out an inference from the rank on each summand’s own cut to a rank bound of two on a common cut. E.4 does not make that inference. The q=3 minor is reproduced in the [check results](../checks/results.json).

### The cost of summing over the cuts

**Local reconstruction.** A.3, p. 19, represents the rank-L contribution for a given S by two auxiliary parties (“hubs”) joined by a dimension-L wire. Only the hubs need to apply functionals in this construction. This satisfies the printed E.7 hypothesis. For J summands, E.7 bounds the spectral value of their sum by J raised to gφ(P), times the largest spectral value of a contributing network. Here P is the set of retained end parties and gφ(P) measures how φ values a shared diagonal selector across them. In the proof, tensor power n gives words of length n in J labels. Group them by multiplicity vector. A shared selector lets all retained parties apply the same permutation within each group. The type count is at most (n+1)^J. For fixed k and J, its nth root tends to one. There is no interchange of limits in J here. See E.7, p. 35. The released theorem permits more general local maps on the retained parties under its sum-of-restrictions and output-support hypotheses. [Formal E.7](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L22)

**Geometric dependency.** E.9 supplies one spectral point φ and one scale c that fit the positions of every candidate hub and end simultaneously, with c gφ(P) ≤ √k + δ. E.8 controls how the exponent changes upon taking midpoints. Starting from cube diagonals of length √k, the proof takes k midpoint steps with a total error bounded by ε. Because J ≤ 2^k, the cost of naming the cut after taking a logarithm and rescaling is at most k(√k + δ). E.5 alone has a threshold depending on k through an uncontrolled scale. E.7–E.9 are what permit k to grow in the quantitative result. See pp. 34–37. [Cube statement](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Convex.lean#L318) · [Use in the rate proof](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Rate.lean#L299)

### Independent check of the numerical margin

For a balanced cut S, the number of crossing edges is |S|(k−|S|) ≥ 2k²/9. Their signed coordinate contributions have a common sign within each coordinate, so the norm of the vector used to place the hubs is at least 2k^(3/2)/9. With η=2/(9√k), the sum of end-to-hub distances saves at least 4k/81 compared with the total length N=k(k−1)/2 of the original edges. In (E.2), set δ=0.001/k and ℓ≥100√k. In units of ℓk the cut-label cost is at most 0.015 and the additive metric errors at most 0.0015. The remaining margin exceeds 1/31, and the hub-to-hub coefficient is at most 0.446/√k. Since 31·0.446<14, log₂ L ≥ ℓk^(3/2)/14. E.10 states the weaker denominator 16. At k=r² and ℓ=100r the bound is of order r⁴, while log₂ m is of order r³ in E.11. This gives the claimed power 4/3 of log₂ m. **Disposition.** The mixed-cut objection is answered for this conditional passage, and we independently recovered its numerical margin. Full validation still depends on the upstream spectral-point extension A.7, curvature C.1, the flat hypothesis where used, and the field dichotomy D.2. This pass did not reconstruct those results end to end. [Original paper, pp. 33–37](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=33)
