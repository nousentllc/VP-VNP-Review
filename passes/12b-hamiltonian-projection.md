# Pass 12B · The grid-to-Hamiltonian-cycle projection

**November 25, 2026.** M.2 gives an exact, weight-preserving bijection between grid assignments and Hamiltonian cycles using the constructed graph’s structural edges. A zero-weight cycle still belongs to the bijection. The affine projection therefore applies in characteristic two, even when different cycles contribute equal monomials and their sum cancels. This resolves the M.2 obligation identified in pass 11D. It supplies a target for the two branch arguments, each of which has its own lower-bound hypotheses. [Manuscript, p. 61](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=61) · [Previous field-transfer audit](11d-field-transfer.md) · [Publisher statement](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/HcGrid.lean#L18)

## Statement, direction and cost

Let the finite incidence set $I$ have two surjections $v:I\to V$ and $w:I\to K$. Here $K$ indexes the second set of bits. Given four weights $W_n(a,b)$ in a commutative ring for each $n$, define

$$
Z=\sum_{x:V\to\{0,1\}}\ \sum_{r:K\to\{0,1\}}\prod_{n\in I} W_n(x_{v(n)},r_{w(n)}).
$$

For every $S\ge40|I|$ the proposition supplies a single $S\times S$ matrix $M$ with entries drawn from $0,1$ and individual $W_n(a,b)$, such that $\mathrm{HC}_S(M)=Z$. The graph depends on the incidence maps, its chosen orderings and $S$. The same structural choices work for every assignment and weight table. Hamiltonian cycles in the polynomial are permutations acting transitively on the vertex set. The cyclic starting point carries no multiplicity. [HC definition](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Rate/Defs.lean#L33) · [Weighted-cycle definition](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Segments.lean#L23) · [Substitution identity](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Grid.lean#L227)

For polynomial weights in the specified affine class, apply the result in the polynomial ring. Each entry of $M$ is constant or affine in at most one variable. The definition allows many occurrences of that variable, even on different incidences. A size-$s$ circuit for generic HC evaluated at these entries gives a circuit of size at most $5s$ for $Z$. Five is the formal allowance for replacing one gate by a block, including constants, the variable, multiplication and addition for a single affine input. Previous outputs remain available to multiple later gates. Hence a lower bound $L$ on the grid circuit gives the constraint $5s\ge L$ on such a candidate HC circuit. The formal flat caller uses this substitution. The contracting caller passes HC’s grid completeness into its own evaluator construction. M.1’s exact preservation of size under changing fields is a different operation. ## Recovering the two bijections

**1. A full cycle chooses one option per segment and one side per XOR.** The segment heads form a cyclic row. Between a head and its successor there is one candidate path for each option. An XOR shared by two options has two tips and one middle vertex. One option enters at the first tip and traverses the middle to the other tip. The second uses the opposite direction. Each tip has two possible exits, to the middle and onwards on the option that arrived from the other side. Only the two tips can precede the middle. [Edge rules](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Segments.lean#L88)

In any structural Hamiltonian cycle the middle has one predecessor and one successor. They must be different tips. Returning to the same tip would close a two-cycle and exclude the other tip. Once the cycle reaches a gadget from a head or a previous gadget, it cannot enter the trailing tip because that vertex already has the middle as predecessor. It therefore follows the selected option through all its XORs in order to the next head. The row of heads is one cycle, so following those choices accounts for its entire orbit. Every gadget must occur on this traversal exactly once. Conversely, choosing one option per head and using exactly one side of every XOR visits every head and every gadget vertex once in row order. This defines one successor permutation. [No two-cycle and forced traversal](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Segments.lean#L297) · [Recovery of the selection](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Segments.lean#L348) · [Transitivity of the constructed route](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Segments.lean#L277)

The first edge leaving each head identifies its option. Distinct options entering gadgets have different entry tips, since a tip belongs to a unique option. The only possible ambiguity is two gadget-free options going directly to the next head. At a variable or row head surjectivity attaches at least one XOR to the true option. Every selector and check option has a gadget. At a flag its false option has a gadget, and a padding segment has one option. Thus there is at most one direct option per head. Formally the weight of an edge from a head is the sum of option weights with that destination. This uniqueness reduces the sum to a single option weight and makes the map from selections to cycle permutations injective. If a surplus bit had no incidence, both of its options would use the same first edge. The construction would then fail its first-edge argument. [First-edge injection](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Segments.lean#L168) · [Grid-specific proof](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Grid.lean#L160) · [Weighted sum and both inverse directions](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Segments.lean#L440)

**2. A valid option selection is uniquely a pair of bit assignments.** For each incidence let $t=(a,b)$ be its selected pair. Each of four flag bits is true exactly when its pair equals $t$, because its false option is linked by XOR to the corresponding selector option. The left check has three options, indexed by $\varnothing,0,1$. Its $d$-option for $d\in\{0,1\}$ is taken exactly when $t=(1,d)$. Its empty option is taken exactly when the shared $x_{v(n)}$ is false. Therefore $a=x_{v(n)}$ and the full left-check choice is forced. The right check gives $b=r_{w(n)}$ by the same calculation with the coordinates exchanged. Reading the choices at the original bit heads recovers $(x,r)$. Conversely those bits force every flag, selector and check, and the resulting choices satisfy all ten XORs at each incidence. Every occurrence of a shared bit is constrained by that same head. [Ten pairs of gadget ports](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Grid.lean#L78) · [Assignment to choices](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Grid.lean#L95) · [Validity](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Grid.lean#L119) · [Uniqueness](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Hc/Grid.lean#L133)

**Weights and zero terms.** Only a selector head carries a potentially nonunit weight, on its first edge. The assignment’s cycle thus has weight exactly $\prod_n W_n(x_{v(n)},r_{w(n)})$. A permutation taking an edge outside the graph contributes zero to the weighted matrix sum. On structural edges, valid cycles can also have weight zero, and equal monomials can cancel when the cycle weights are added. The bijection is made before either operation. Its formal combinatorial core works over any commutative semiring. ## Size, padding and conventions at small sizes

The row has $|V|+|K|+7|I|$ unpadded heads, including one selector, four flags and two checks per incidence. There are $4+3+3=10$ XORs per incidence, each with three additional vertices. The unpadded total is $B=|V|+|K|+37|I|\le39|I|$. For every $S\ge40|I|$ append exactly $S-B$ unit-weight one-option segments and choose a cyclic row order. Each assignment has a unique forced route through the added heads. Relabeling vertices by $\{0,\ldots,S-1\}$ leaves all cycle weights unchanged. With $I=\varnothing$ both index sets are empty by surjectivity, so the grid sum is one. At $S=1$ the padded graph has a unit self-loop. At $S=0$ the formal transitivity predicate on the empty vertex set admits the empty permutation, whose empty product is one. The asymptotic circuit argument never needs to choose this convention at large $S$. A separate search fixes one start vertex and enumerates simple directed paths that return there after visiting every vertex. Its pruning only removes paths that would leave a vertex without any possible predecessor. It imposes no XOR or assignment constraints during the search. It compares the resulting multiset of incidence-weight monomials with direct assignment enumeration. Seven configurations with two slot orderings each included sizes 0, 1, 4, 40, 43 and 80. Every incidence-weight monomial had multiplicity one before any identification of weights. [Executable finite check](../checks/pass12b_cycles.py)
- A polynomial control tests the risk of splitting a shared bit pair into independent copies. Give weight $z$ to $(1,1)$ at the first incidence and $(1,0)$ at the second, and give all other entries weight one. The shared pair gives $2+2z$. Independent copies give $(3+z)^2$. In $\mathbb F_2[z]$ these are $0$ and $1+z^2$. If both triggers are instead $(1,1)$, the integer answers are $3+z^2$ and $(3+z)^2$, which agree modulo two. Checking only the latter specialization would miss the error. [Polynomial and collision controls](../checks/pass12b_cycles.py)
