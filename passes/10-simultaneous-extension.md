# Pass 10 · One price extension for all pairs

**November 25, 2026.** Propositions A.7 and A.8 supply the simultaneous extension needed by the C.1 fan and the C.3 midpoint construction. The argument has two separate ways of keeping the witnesses together. On a finite spectral price, a single tensor product records every old wire distance, and a single spectral point obeys the upper bound on every wire. For a general price, a limit is taken over whole distance tables. We reconstructed both steps from the manuscript and the pinned formal source. [A.7 and A.8, pp. 21–22](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=21) · [Fan caller](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Fan.lean#L60) · [Midpoint caller](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Curvature.lean#L37)

## Statements and hypotheses

A spectral point $\varphi$ assigns a nonnegative real value to each tensor, takes the unit to one, is multiplicative on tensor products, and bounds the value of a covered tensor by the sum of the values of its covering tensors. For the binary wire $W_{uv}=\langle 2\rangle_{uv}$ its value is $2^{d_\varphi(u,v)}$. A price on a finite set is a limit of nonnegative multiples of spectral distances, with one multiplier and one spectral point approximating **all pairs** at each accuracy. On arbitrary sets its finite pullbacks must be prices over the same field. Appendix A uses real coefficients under the convention on p. 18. The formal A.7 and A.8 are parameterized by an arbitrary field $K$. The exponent base two is a positive real number and the binary wire has two index values. [Field and tensor conventions, p. 18](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=18) · [A.1 and A.2, p. 19](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=19) · [Price definition](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L23)

- **A.7.** Fix finite party sets, an inclusion $\iota:I\to I'$, finitely many retractions $\pi_l:I'\to I$ with $\pi_l\iota=\mathrm{id}$, weights $\theta_l\ge0$ summing to one, a spectral point $\varphi$ on $I$, and an old tensor $C$. There exists **one** spectral point $\varphi'$ on $I'$ satisfying $\varphi'(t)\le \prod_l\varphi((\pi_l)_*t)^{\theta_l}$ for every enlarged tensor $t$, and equality $\varphi'(\iota_*C)=\varphi(C)$ at the chosen tensor. The witness may depend on $C$. [A.7](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Extension.lean#L166)
- **A.8.** Fix a $K$-price $w$ on $Y$, disjoint new labels $Q$, and one joint random map $r_\omega:Y\sqcup Q\to Y$ fixing $Y$. Each new label has finite range, with measurable singleton fibers on a common probability space. Then there is **one** $K$-price $w'$ with $w'(y,y')=w(y,y')$ on all old pairs and $w'(a,b)\le B(a,b):=\mathbb E[w(r_\omega a,r_\omega b)]$ on all pairs. The expectation uses the same outcome for both labels. For infinite $Q$, the whole map can have infinite range as a random variable. [A.8](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L366) · [Finite-law interface used by the callers](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L414)

## How A.7 obtains one spectral witness

1. **A common decomposition bounds every cover.** Write $f(t)=\prod_l\varphi((\pi_l)_*t)^{\theta_l}$. Push-forward and spectral monotonicity make $f$ multiplicative and monotone under restriction. Let $p(t)$ be the infimum of $\sum_j f(b_j)$ over finite covers of $t$. For a cover of $\iota_*A$, write the sum of restricted terms in the target index spaces. The old tensor has a singleton index at each added party. Thus those same terms come from old tensors $a_j$ covering $A$, and $a_j$ is a restriction of $(\pi_l)_*b_j$ for every $l$. The order matters: the $a_j$ are selected before $l$. Each $\varphi(a_j)$ is bounded by all the factors’ bases and hence by their normalized weighted geometric mean. Consequently $\varphi(A)\le\sum_j f(b_j)$. [Cover descent](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Extension.lean#L44) · [Geometric-mean bound](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Extension.lean#L69)
2. **The gauge agrees on all old tensors.** The one-term cover gives $p\le f$ and $f(\iota_*A)=\varphi(A)$ by retraction and normalization. The preceding lower bound gives $p(\iota_*A)=\varphi(A)$ for each $A$. Besides normalizing $p(1)$, this verifies the exact values on the powers of $\iota_*C$ needed next. At a zero-valued old tensor the product is zero because some weight is positive. Zero-weight factors do not affect the product. [Retraction equality](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Extension.lean#L84) · [Cover gauge](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Extension.lean#L105)
3. **A.5 preserves the selected equality when passing to a spectral point.** Put $c=\varphi(C)$. For $c>0$ let $q(t)=\inf_{m\ge0}p(t(\iota_*C)^m)/c^m$. The power identities give a normalized gauge $q\le p$ satisfying $q(t)\le q(t\iota_*C)/c$ for every $t$. A.5 provides a spectral point below this gauge that respects the same inequality. Inserting $t=1$ gives $c\le\varphi'(\iota_*C)$, while domination by $p$ gives the reverse. For $c=0$, nonnegativity and domination already force equality. The manuscript’s case split at the zero tensor gives the same conclusion because a nonzero tensor has spectral value at least one. A.5’s selection of a spectral point is an input to this mathematical reconstruction. [A.5, p. 20](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=20) · [Localization](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Gauges.lean#L315) · [Selection preserving the inequality](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Gauges.lean#L392) · [A.7 endpoint](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Extension.lean#L166)

## Why one chosen equality fixes every old distance

For finite old set $P$ and new set $Q_0$, let $r_l$ be the possible joint placements, extended by the identity on $P$. Apply A.7 **once**, with
$$
 C=\prod_{(u,v)\in P\times P}W_{uv}.
$$
The formal product includes both orientations and the diagonal. For its single witness $\varphi'$, apply the universal domination to $W_{ab}$ for arbitrary $a,b\in P\sqcup Q_0$. The wire formula and monotonicity of $2^x$ give
$$
 d_{\varphi'}(a,b)\le\sum_l\theta_l\,d_\varphi(r_l a,r_l b).
$$
This already bounds every old–new and new–new distance. On an old pair the right side is exactly $d_\varphi(u,v)$. Define the slack $s_{uv}=d_\varphi(u,v)-d_{\varphi'}(\iota u,\iota v)\ge0$. Multiplicativity on $C$ and A.7’s equality at $C$ give equality of two positive powers of two, hence $\sum_{u,v}s_{uv}=0$. A finite sum of nonnegative terms can vanish only if each term vanishes. This proves all the old equalities for the very same witness that gave all the upper bounds. Zero old distances and repeated labels cause no loss. [A.8, p. 22](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=22) · [Single invocation, universal wire bound and equality of sums](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L160)

**Independent control for the choice of $C$.** On two parties take the identity retraction. Matrix rank and the indicator of a nonzero matrix are spectral points. The indicator is bounded by rank everywhere and agrees with it on the unit. On the binary wire, represented by the $2\times2$ identity, the two values are 1 and 2. Their wire distances are therefore 0 and 1. A witness that saturates the unit can lose an old distance. The example pinpoints the role of the all-wire product without assuming A.8 or C.1. The accompanying check enumerates the 16 binary $2\times2$ matrices over $\mathbb F_2$ for this comparison. [Spectral examples, p. 19](../primary/vp-vnp/paper.pdf#page=19) · [Exact checks](../checks/pass10_examples.py)

## How the inequalities survive both compactness steps

**Finite point set, general price.** For $\epsilon_n=1/(n+1)$ choose a single $s_n\ge0$ and $\varphi_n$ approximating $w$ uniformly on the old table within $\epsilon_n$. Extend $\varphi_n$ by the preceding construction and put $d_n=s_n d_{\varphi'_n}$. Every old coordinate of $d_n$ is within $\epsilon_n$ of $w$. For each pair on the enlarged set,
$$
 0\le d_n(a,b)\le\sum_l\theta_l\big(w(r_l a,r_l b)+\epsilon_n\big)
   = B(a,b)+\epsilon_n .
$$
Normalization gives one error of size $\epsilon_n$ regardless of the number of pairs or placements. No division by $s_n$ is required. All $d_n$ belong to the compact set of prices bounded coordinatewise by $B+1$. A single cluster limit of the entire table is a price, agrees exactly with $w$ on old pairs, and is bounded by $B$ everywhere. Lean takes one ultrafilter extending the tail filter on the approximation indices. [Compactness of bounded prices](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L100) · [Approximation, scale and error](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L259) · [One limit, both conclusions](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L289)

**Arbitrary old and new sets.** Start with a finite test set $S\subseteq Y\sqcup Q$. Its new labels form a finite set $T$. Include the old labels of $S$ and every possible image of $T$ in one finite old set. The finitely many possible *joint* maps on $T$ give a probability list: their events are finite intersections of measurable coordinate fibers. This one list computes the expectation of every function of the placement, hence all the required pair bounds on $S$. The finite case now supplies a local price. [Joint finite law](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L316) · [Finite enlargement and pullback](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L383)

Choose one such local price for each finite set. They need not agree on overlaps. For each pair the proof extends local tables by zero outside their domains to define one real-valued function on the finite test sets. It then uses one ultrafilter on finite sets containing every tail of supersets. For a fixed finite $S$, along that tail each restriction to $S$ is a price bounded by $B$. Compactness yields a limit on $S$. On overlapping finite sets, each shared coordinate is the limit of the same real-valued function along the same ultrafilter, so uniqueness of the real limit forces agreement. The resulting global table is a $K$-price by the finite-restriction criterion. The witness at this stage is the price itself. Its finite pullbacks have the spectral approximations required by Definition A.4. On old pairs the lower bound is $w$ and the upper bound $B$ also equals $w$. On the remaining pairs the lower bound is zero and the upper bound is $B$. This construction needs neither a countable enumeration of all labels nor a tensor product over infinitely many old wires. [Common filter, compatible coordinate limits](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L210) · [Two-sided constraints](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Prices.lean#L373)

**Independent checks on the quantifiers.** Place a new label alternately at two old points at distance one. The two pullback prices give the new label distance profiles $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$. Choosing a separate subsequence to minimize each coordinate gives $(0,0)$, violating the old triangle inequality. This example tests the need for a common limiting choice. Also, two fair labels moving together have expected mutual distance 0, while two fair labels swapping places have expected mutual distance 1 on the same old space. Their marginals coincide. The input to A.8 includes the joint law. The pass-8 example further shows how a new–new upper bound can be strict: both swapped labels must be midpoints and C.1 forces their distance to be zero. That last observation uses C.1 downstream and serves only to interpret the bound. [Exact controls](../checks/pass10_examples.py) · [Earlier shared-coin example](../checks/pass08_midpoints.py)
