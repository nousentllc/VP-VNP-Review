## B.1 · Why the supremum is also the limit

Write $`P(E)`$ for the count of ordered pairs $`(I,J)`$ of finite subsets of the nonnegative integers with equal cardinality and combined odd-part energy at most $`E`$. Put $`P(0)=1`$. For $`k`$ inputs use residues $`r`$ for $`I_r`$ and $`k-1-r`$ for $`J_r`$ modulo $`k`$. The contribution of the first half relative to $`k`$ times its original energy is $`(2r+1-k)|I_r|`$. The other half contributes its negative because the cardinalities agree. Reduction modulo $`k`$ recovers each input and hence $`P(k^2E)\ge P(E)^k`$. Using residue $`r`$ on both sides already gives energy six in place of four when $`k=2`$ and only the second input is $`(\{0\},\{0\})`$. [Counting and interleaving](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/States.lean#L26)

For $`E\ge1`$, the number of possible individual subsets is at most $`t^{-E}\prod_{i<E}(1+t^{2i+1})`$ for $`0<t<1`$. Its logarithm is at most $`-E\log t+(1-t)^{-1}`$. At $`t=1-1/(2\sqrt E)`$ the inequality $`-\log(1-z)\le2z`$ for $`0\le z\le1/2`$ bounds this by $`3\sqrt E`$. Squaring the count gives $`\log P(E)\le6\sqrt E`$, sufficient for the paper's looser coefficient ten. Let $`\kappa=\sup_{E\ge1}\log P(E)/\sqrt E`$. It is finite, positive since $`P(2)=2`$, and gives the upper bound for **every** energy. For $`0<\eta<\kappa`$ fix one integer $`D`$ with $`\log P(D)>(\kappa-\eta)\sqrt D`$. For any sufficiently large $`E`$, choose $`k=\lfloor\sqrt{E/D}\rfloor`$. By monotonicity and interleaving,
```math
\frac{\log P(E)}{\sqrt E}\ge (\kappa-\eta)\left(1-\sqrt{D/E}\right).
```
First let $`E`$ tend to infinity with $`D`$ fixed, then send $`\eta`$ to zero. This proves the all-energy ceiling and the limit. [Upper envelope](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/States.lean#L58) · [Formal B.1](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/States.lean#L136)

**Independent normalization.** A partition with $`d`$ diagonal boxes has strictly decreasing arm lengths and leg lengths there. Those two lists determine the diagram uniquely by putting back its disjoint diagonal hooks. Their area is $`d+\sum I+\sum J`$, exactly half the symbol energy. For $`t>0`$, counting partitions by the multiplicity of each part gives the generating function for exact energy
```math
Z(t)=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-e^{-2tj})^{-1},\qquad
t\log Z(t)=\sum_{h\ge1}\frac{t}{h(e^{2th}-1)}\longrightarrow\frac12\sum_{h\ge1}h^{-2}=\frac{\pi^2}{12}.
```
The interchange uses nonnegative terms, and $`1/(2h^2)`$ dominates each term for the limit. To recover $`\kappa`$, use $`Z(t)\ge e^{-tE}P(E)`$ with $`E=\lfloor(\kappa/(2t))^2\rfloor`$, giving $`\liminf_{t\downarrow0}t\log Z(t)\ge\kappa^2/4`$ by B.1. For any $`0<\delta<1`$, the exact count at $`e`$ is at most $`P(e)`$. Completing the square gives $`\kappa\sqrt e-(1-\delta)te\le\kappa^2/[4(1-\delta)t]`$. Summing $`e^{-\delta te}`$ contributes $`1/(1-e^{-\delta t})`$, whose logarithm times $`t`$ tends to zero. Letting $`\delta\downarrow0`$ gives the matching upper bound. Thus **$`\kappa=\pi/\sqrt3`$** with natural logs and this energy convention. This evaluation is a review derivation. B.3 uses the same positive $`\kappa`$ for both wires and does not require its value. ## B.2 · A coefficient factors before and after reflection

Let $`y=X^2`$ and $`\beta_r(u)=(1+u)^r/(1+yu)^{r+1}`$. The term $`y^r\beta_r(u)\beta_r(v)`$ multiplied by $`(1-y)(1-yuv)`$ is $`H_r-H_{r+1}`$, where $`H_r=y^r(1+yu)(1+yv)\beta_r(u)\beta_r(v)`$. This follows by expanding $`(1+yu)(1+yv)-y(1+u)(1+v)`$. Since $`H_0=1`$ and $`H_R`$ is divisible by $`y^R`$, taking the coefficient of $`u^iv^j`$ after dividing by $`1-yuv`$ gives display (B.1). The division is in the formal series ring with constant term one. Thus, in the manuscript's $`F D G`$ factorization, the difficult $`(d,b)`$ entry agrees with its signed diagonal modulo $`X^{2R}`$. The other three entries use the delta factors to select an index below $`R`$, or have a missing term already divisible by $`X^{2R}`$. Transposition with the swapped halves supplies the lower block with its minus sign. Entrywise congruence passes to determinants by their polynomial formula. Choosing $`R=N+1`$ preserves coefficient $`N`$. [Orthogonality](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Identity.lean#L112) · [Block congruence](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Identity.lean#L186) · [Determinant congruence](../primary/vp-vnp/Nc/Cross/Psi.lean#L81)

Here is an explicit sign computation over any commutative ring for the **printed** convention. Let the two diagonal blocks be $`A`$ and $`B`$ of sizes $`m,n`$, with off-diagonal blocks $`FDG`$ and $`-G'DF'`$. Append the two groups of $`R`$ auxiliary rows and columns of p. 24. The lower diagonal submatrix is $`\mathrm{diag}(-I_R,I_R)`$, so its determinant contributes $`(-1)^R`$ on passing back to the original matrix. Choose the nondelta rows $`U,V`$ in the two auxiliary groups. Deleting the other rows with their corresponding columns contributes $`(-1)^{R-|U|}`$ from the surviving choices of $`-1`$. With natural orders retained within subsets, regrouping the rows from $`(m,n,U,V)`$ to $`(m,V,n,U)`$ and columns to $`(m,U,n,V)`$ contributes $`(-1)^{|V|(n+|U|)+n|U|}`$. Rectangular unmatched blocks force the term to vanish unless $`|U|=|V|=d`$. Its combined sign exponent then has parity $`d^2-d=0`$. Consequently
```math
\det\begin{pmatrix} A&FDG\\-G'DF'&B\end{pmatrix}
=\sum_{|U|=|V|}
X^{e(U,V)}
\det\begin{pmatrix}A&F_{:,U}\\ F'_{V,:}&0\end{pmatrix}
\det\begin{pmatrix}B&G'_{:,V}\\ G_{U,:}&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad D_{rr}=X^{2r+1}.
```
The determinants of the two bordered matrices retain their own signs. Removing the minus on the original lower off-diagonal block already changes $`+1`$ to $`-1`$ in the scalar purely off-diagonal example. The formal proof uses an equivalent augmented matrix with identity auxiliary block and absorbs signs into its separated functions. [Schur form and row expansion](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Identity.lean#L455) · [Unequal counts](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Identity.lean#L485) · [Product factors](../primary/vp-vnp/Paper/Identity.lean#L497)

For coefficient $`N`$, each surviving $`(U,V)`$ is an actual symbol of energy at most $`N`$. Its remaining degree $`N-e(U,V)`$ is split into two nonnegative degrees in at most $`N+1`$ ways. Each integer term has its first factor depending on $`(a,d)`$ and its second on $`(b,c)`$. This gives $`(N+1)P(N)`$ products. Here $`a,c`$ come from one fixed size-and-energy class and $`b,d`$ from another. Reflection swaps $`b,d`$, preserves the determinant sign under their simultaneous row/column permutation, and multiplies the reciprocal determinant by $`X^\Theta`$ after cancelling the common even power of $`1-X^2`$. This is a Laurent polynomial identity obtained with $`X`$ indeterminate. It establishes the second bound at $`\Theta-N`$ for the same coefficient and specializes to every field afterwards. At $`X=1`$ the equal-size block swap has sign $`(-1)^{(\mu+\nu)^2}`$ and the two triangular determinants contribute $`(-1)^{\mu+\nu}`$ together in the specified ordering, giving the matching indicator after relabeling. ## General result, finite controls and scope

In B.3 the two large input classes use the lower growth estimate at the chosen input energies. A zero input weight has a one-element zero-energy class. The possibly varying output index $`M`$ needs only the all-energy ceiling $`\log((M+1)P(M))\le\kappa\sqrt M+\log(M+1)`$. Since $`M\le\Theta\le 2(p^2+q^2)n`$ at fixed weights, the overhead divided by $`\kappa\sqrt n`$ vanishes uniformly, including at $`M=0`$. This explains why the same coefficient can pay for both cuts even near an endpoint. Local choices of $`N`$ may still differ by field or scale.
