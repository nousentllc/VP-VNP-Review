\subhead{What the review can establish}
Claude (Anthropic) claims a superpolynomial lower bound for every arithmetic circuit computing the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial over each field. This review traces where the proof admits an arbitrary circuit, how it preserves that circuit's value, and which estimates eventually exclude it. Pass 11 reconstructed four links serving different parts of the argument.

\textbf{Geometry.} One coefficient of the crossing-wire determinant supports both cut estimates. Its degree and complementary degree divide a single budget. The resulting inequality is strong enough to constrain the averaged lengths in the curvature proof.

\textbf{Spectral selection.} A.5 selects one multiplicative functional retaining every comparison that holds at all tensor multipliers. The flat branch uses that choice to keep all original wire distances and a designated unit-tensor exponent together.

\textbf{Weighted computation.} Cutting a network at a fixed good outcome preserves its scalar value when the pieces agree on each crossing packet ID. The amount of data they share depends separately on the shape and load of the tree supplied by H.2.

\textbf{Field constants.} A candidate circuit uses finitely many constants. A simultaneous specialization preserves their relations and every gate, at the same list length. This extends a bound from an algebraically closed field to all fields of that characteristic.

\subhead{Where the conclusion stands}
These results explain specific steps in the authors' claimed separation. The full independent reconstruction still depends on the contracting laws and the reductions to the target polynomial. Pages 2--6 give the circuit and curvature baseline. Pages 7--10 give the four new derivations. Pages 11--12 distinguish the available evidence from the next proof obligations.

\heading{Where every circuit enters}
For each $m$, take a generic $m\times m$ matrix $X=(X_{ij})$ of independent variables over a field. The Hamiltonian cycle polynomial $\HC_m$ sums $\prod_i X_{i,\sigma(i)}$ over permutations $\sigma$ consisting of one cycle. The permanent $\Perm_m$ takes the same sum over all permutations.

Theorem 1.1 asserts that some absolute $c>0$ works as follows. For each characteristic $p$, there is $m_0(p)$ such that for every field of that characteristic, every $m\ge m_0(p)$, and every circuit computing $\HC_m$,
\[
  \operatorname{size}(C)>m^{\,c(\log_2 m)^{1/3}} .
\]
The stated bound also applies to $\Perm_m$ when $p\ne2$. The circuit is a list of variables, field constants and binary sums or products of earlier entries. Size counts all entries. A gate may be reused, and the target must occur as a formal polynomial. This allows nonuniform circuits, unrestricted depth, cancellation and field-dependent constants. The paper treats approximation separately.

\subhead{D.2 splits the argument by field}
\textbf{Flat branch (passes 1--2).} E.4 starts with an arbitrary circuit for the auxiliary polynomial. Its decomposition permits up to the circuit size in products \emph{per balanced subset}. Different summands can use different cuts. E.7 pays for recombining them, while E.9 supplies a single spectral point for all candidate cuts. Fixing one cut in advance would lose coverage.

\textbf{Contracting branch (passes 3--6).} K.2 turns an arbitrary circuit into a program computing the sum of accepting-run weights. I.1 preserves that value and the stated exact-time evaluation once the time threshold is met. The interpreter can discard a zero-weight accepting branch for an unsupported name. The proofs using this construction consume the sum. J.2--J.4 use a bounded outcome sum and account for constructing and concatenating the piece lists. M.1--M.4 handle field transfer and the Hamiltonian-cycle substitution.

\textbf{Shared geometry (passes 7--10).} D.2 uses simultaneous rank averages, nonnegative defects, rank rigidity and the price-space cube. Requiring only a witness $m$ for each test point $z$ in the squared inequality would be vacuous, since $m=z$ always works. C.3 supplies one midpoint for all $z$ in the complete space. C.1 derives the curvature coefficient, and A.8 supplies the common price in which its fan is constructed.

The original QFTN and Uriel-GEMA audit motivated the question of how a local construction can constrain every circuit. Here the universal quantifier enters through the two circuit transformations and the field alternative covering their cases. Pass 11 follows four specific operations inside that chain. Its field-transfer argument explains why even circuit-dependent constants are covered.

\heading{Curvature from four fan estimates}
A $K$-price is a pseudometric whose restriction to any finite configuration can be approximated on all pairs at once by one nonnegative multiple of a spectral-point distance over $K$. Assume $w(x,m),w(v,m)\le D$ and $w(x,v)=2D$. The triangle inequality forces both legs through $m$ to have length $D$. Set $b=w(z,m)$ and
\[
 \Gamma=b^2-\tfrac12\bigl(w(z,x)^2+w(z,v)^2\bigr),\qquad \theta=1/T.
\]

\subhead{The fan and its averaged lengths}
At fixed $T\ge2$ and fan size $n$, A.8 first supplies two chains from $x$ and $v$ to $m$ using a common random choice. A second shared choice supplies points between each chain point $\gamma_i$ and $z$. Write $L_i=w_{\rm fan}(z,\gamma_i)$. The fan extends the original price, and
$|L_{i+1}-L_i|\le D/n^2$, $0\le L_i\le D+b$. Distances to $z$ along a spoke equal $\rho L_i$, with $\rho=1-j/(Tn^2)$ at level $j$. Equality here follows from the two endpoint upper bounds and the triangle inequality.

Pull back the price scaled by $n^2$ to the two lattice sheets. Average its squared lengths in $n^2$-by-$n$ boxes over both sheets with the translation-invariant limiting mean. Let $\nu(g)$ be the resulting root-mean-square length and
$N(g)=\inf_{S\ge1}\nu(Sg)/S$. Put $\Lambda_2=\mathbb E[L_i^2]\le(D+b)^2$. At this $T$, the four fan estimates are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 N(1,0)&\ge D,\\
 N(1,-1)^2&\le D^2(1-\theta+\theta^2),\\
 N(0,1)^2&\le\theta^2\Lambda_2,\\
 N(1,-1-A)^2&\ge A^2\theta^2\Lambda_2
      +A\theta\Gamma-4A\theta^2D(D+b).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first bound telescopes the two sheets to their common end. For the next two bounds a single step suffices. The last bound must hold for the normalized length at \emph{every} positive $S$ before taking the infimum.

\subhead{Where the explicit error comes from}
Hold $A,S,T$ fixed and take $n$ large enough that $AS\le n$. Across an $S$-step displacement,
$n^2|L_{i+S}^2-L_i^2|\le2SD(D+b)$ and $1-\rho\le2\theta$ in the averaging window. Removing $\rho$ from the squared cross term therefore costs at most $4AS^2\theta^2D(D+b)$. Normalization divides by $S^2$. For clamped indices, the finite-box average of the squared-length difference times $n^2$ differs from $S\Gamma$ by at most $D(D+b)S(S-1)/n^2$. This discrepancy vanishes for each fixed $S$.

\heading{Why the residual vanishes}
For each fixed $T$, one family of fans and one limiting mean give the same $N$ and $\Lambda_2$ for all $A,S$. The box sizes grow first with displacements held fixed. Ordinary convergence of every box average is unnecessary. Subadditivity of $\nu$ then identifies $N(g)$ with the ordinary large-$S$ limit of $\nu(Sg)/S$.

\subhead{First remove the displacement at fixed $T$}
Lemma C.2 makes the second difference of $f(\ell)=N(1,-1-\ell)^2$ equal $2N(0,1)^2$. Thus $\psi(\ell)=f(\ell)-\ell^2\theta^2\Lambda_2$ has nonpositive second differences. Its integer slopes decrease, so
\[
 \psi(A)\le\psi(0)+A\bigl(\psi(0)-\psi(-1)\bigr).
\]
Insert the four estimates. After division by $A\theta>0$ the only intercept term is $N(1,-1)^2/(A\theta)$. It tends to zero as $A\to\infty$ at the fixed $T$. In the original normalization the resulting bound is
\[
 \theta\Gamma-4\theta^2 D(D+b)
 \ \le\ D^2(-\theta+\theta^2)+\theta^2\Lambda_2 .
\]

\subhead{Then remove the mesh parameter}
Divide by $\theta>0$ and use $\Lambda_2\le(D+b)^2$. This gives
\[
 \Gamma+D^2 \le \frac{D^2+(D+b)^2+4D(D+b)}{T}
     =\frac{6D^2+6Db+b^2}{T}.
\]
Each $T$ can have its own fan and averaged lengths. The numerator depends only on the original points, so $T\to\infty$ gives $\Gamma\le-D^2$. A joint limit such as $A=T$ would leave $1/(A\theta)=1$ and would not justify the intercept step. The successive estimates avoid that interchange.

\heading{How a single tensor fixes every old wire}
\textbf{A.7 in finite sets.} Let $\iota:I\to I'$ be the inclusion of parties, let $\pi_\ell:I'\to I$ be retractions with $\pi_\ell\iota=\mathrm{id}$, and choose weights $\theta_\ell\ge0$ summing to one. Fix a spectral point $\varphi$ on $I$ and \emph{one} old tensor $C$. Proposition A.7 gives a spectral point $\varphi'$ on $I'$ satisfying
\[
 \varphi'(t)\le f(t):=\prod_\ell\varphi(\pi_\ell t)^{\theta_\ell}
 \quad\text{for every }t,\qquad \varphi'(\iota C)=\varphi(C).
\]
The old tensor is chosen before the witness. For a different choice of $C$ the existence statement may select a different $\varphi'$.

\subhead{Why the tight bound can be attained}
The proof sets $p(t)=\inf_{\text{covers }(b_j)\text{ of }t}\sum_j f(b_j)$. A cover writes the tensor as a sum of restrictions of the covering tensors. A cover of $\iota A$ induces a cover of $A$ with each summand restricted from every corresponding $\pi_\ell b_j$. Thus $\varphi(A)\le\sum_j f(b_j)$ for each cover. The one-term cover gives the reverse bound because $\pi_\ell\iota=\mathrm{id}$. The resulting gauge $p\le f$ agrees with $\varphi$ on \emph{every} old tensor.

To extract a spectral point attaining equality on the designated $C$, put $c=\varphi(C)$. For $c>0$ localize the gauge using $q(t)=\inf_{r\ge0}p(t\,(\iota C)^r)/c^r$. The equality $p((\iota C)^r)=c^r$ permits this normalization. A.5 selects a spectral point below $q$ retaining the constraint $1\le\varphi'(\iota C)/c$. The upper bound is $c$. When $c=0$, domination and nonnegativity already force equality.

\subhead{A.8 chooses the tensor that encodes all the constraints}
For a finite old set $P$ take $C=\prod_{u,v\in P}\langle2\rangle_{uv}$, over all \emph{ordered} pairs, including the diagonal. The binary wire has spectral value $2^{d_\varphi(u,v)}$. Apply A.7 once. On each enlarged wire $(a,b)$, monotonicity of $\log_2$ turns its product bound into $d_{\varphi'}(a,b)\le\sum_\ell\theta_\ell d_\varphi(\pi_\ell a,\pi_\ell b)$. Old pairs are fixed by each placement and hence their deficits are nonnegative. Multiplicativity and $\varphi'(\iota C)=\varphi(C)$ give
\[
 \sum_{u,v\in P}\bigl[d_\varphi(u,v)-d_{\varphi'}(\iota u,\iota v)\bigr]=0 .
\]
Each term is zero. This accounts for all old distances while leaving only upper bounds on pairs involving a new label.

\subhead{What would fail for a different choice of tensor}
On two parties use the identity retraction, take $\varphi$ to be matrix rank, and let $\psi$ equal one on nonzero matrices and zero on the zero matrix. Both are spectral points; $\psi\le\varphi$ and they agree on the unit $C=1$. Yet $\psi(I_2)=1$ and $\varphi(I_2)=2$, so the old binary-wire distance drops from one to zero. Agreement on a single arbitrary test tensor gives no general wire preservation. The manuscript presents Appendices A--L over the reals (p.~18); the formal A.7 and A.8 declarations quantify over a field $K$.

\heading{From a finite witness to one global price}
\subhead{The finite approximation pays the error once}
For finite old and new sets, let $E(a,b)=\sum_\ell\theta_\ell w(\pi_\ell a,\pi_\ell b)$. By the price definition, for every $n$ one $\varphi_n$ and one scale $s_n\ge0$ approximate \emph{all} old entries within $\varepsilon_n=1/(n+1)$. Extend $\varphi_n$ as on page 5 and put $d_n=s_n d_{\varphi'_n}$. An old coordinate is within $\varepsilon_n$ of its original value. For all enlarged pairs,
\[
 d_n(a,b)\le \sum_\ell\theta_\ell
   \bigl[w(\pi_\ell a,\pi_\ell b)+\varepsilon_n\bigr]=E(a,b)+\varepsilon_n .
\]
The weights sum to one, so the error is independent of the number of maps and pairs. The price tables bounded by $E+1$ form a compact set. One cofinal ultrafilter on $n$ gives a cluster price for the whole table. Sending $\varepsilon_n$ to zero gives exact old coordinates and all the upper bounds.

\subhead{Arbitrary label sets share one limiting choice}
For any finite test set $S\subseteq Y\sqcup Q$, take its new labels and adjoin all their possible placements to its old labels. This gives finite sets because each new label has finite range. On the common probability space, the realized \emph{joint} placement on these new labels takes finitely many values. Its event weights preserve dependence between coordinates. The finite result gives a price on $S$ between a lower table (old $w$ on old pairs and zero elsewhere) and the expected-distance table $E$.

Finite witnesses need not agree in advance. Order the finite test sets by inclusion and take one ultrafilter containing every inclusion tail. On each finite restriction compactness gives a limiting price. Two restrictions containing the same pair take the limit of the same real coordinate along this ultrafilter. Uniqueness of that limit makes the tables agree on overlaps. Their common function is a price by the finite-restriction definition and satisfies every bound simultaneously.

\textbf{Why equality on new pairs would fail.} Take two old points a unit apart. A fair coin assigns two new labels to opposite endpoints, then swaps them. Each label's distance to each old endpoint is at most $1/2$, hence exactly $1/2$ by the triangle inequality. Both labels are midpoints of the old pair; C.1 forces their mutual distance to zero, although its expected value under placement is one.

\heading{11A. One coefficient controls both cuts}
The product of a wire joining $a$ to $c$ and one joining $b$ to $d$ has entries $[a=c][b=d]$. Proposition B.2 writes it as a sum of integral coefficient tensors $\Psi_N$, $0\le N\le\Theta$, for $\Theta=2(e_1+e_2)$. Here $e_1,e_2$ are the fixed energies of the two wire alphabets. Each coefficient admits two product decompositions, with $P(M)$ counting Frobenius symbols of energy at most $M$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{partition of parties}&\text{number of products at most}&\text{interior hubs}\\ \hline
AD\mid BC &(N+1)P(N)&w,w'\\
AB\mid CD &(\Theta-N+1)P(\Theta-N)&o,o'
\end{array}
\]
Near $X=0$, the determinant expansion counts the products for $AD\mid BC$. Reflecting $X\mapsto X^{-1}$ and exchanging $b,d$ gives the complementary degree and the second cut. The identity is integral, so the coefficients and both decompositions can be mapped into the working field.

\subhead{The estimate is joint before taking limits}
For weights $p,q\ge0$ choose alphabet energy caps $\lfloor p^2 n\rfloor,\lfloor q^2 n\rfloor$. Class log sizes divided by $\kappa\sqrt n$ approach $p,q$ by B.1. For a fixed spectral point, the value of the wire product is at most $\sum_N\varphi(\Psi_N)$. A maximizing term therefore loses at most $\log(\Theta+1)$ in the logarithm. Apply both hub bounds to that same term. The resulting weights obey
\[
 r^2=\frac{\Theta-N}{n},\qquad (r')^2=\frac{N}{n},\qquad
 r^2+(r')^2\le2(p^2+q^2).
\]
The logarithmic overhead divided by $\kappa\sqrt n$ tends to zero uniformly in the chosen degree. Compactness gives one limiting pair satisfying both bounds, first for spectral distances and then for a price obtained by approximating all eight labels. Adding the two bounds and applying Young's inequality removes $r,r'$. C.2 applies the resulting display (B.2) at each lattice translate, with $p,q$ equal to the local wire lengths. Cauchy--Schwarz controls the mixed products after averaging.

\subhead{A cut-by-cut choice changes the conclusion}
At the corners of the square $[-1,1]^2$ in the Manhattan metric, use the two diagonals as wires and opposite edge midpoints as the two pairs of hubs. For $p=q=1$, the total wire demand is eight, each outer-leg sum is four, and each hub gap is two. Satisfying both cuts forces $r,r'\ge2$, exceeding the squared budget of four. Choosing separate pairs $(2,0)$ and $(0,2)$ would conceal the failure. The scalar display at $t_0=1$ would read $16\le14$.

\heading{11B. A spectral point keeps all the comparisons}
An upper functional $p_0$ takes the tensor unit to one, is nonnegative and submultiplicative, and bounds a covered tensor by the sum of the values of the cover. Say it keeps $(x,y,c)$, for $c\ge0$, if $p_0(tx)\le c\,p_0(ty)$ for every tensor multiplier $t$. Proposition A.5 selects a single spectral point $\varphi\le p_0$ keeping every such triple. Its additional property is exact multiplicativity.

\subhead{Why the selected functional is multiplicative}
Order the upper functionals below $p_0$ that keep all of its triples by pointwise comparison. In a descending chain, finitely many evaluations can approach their infima at the same chain member. This passes each finite-cover and submultiplicative inequality to the pointwise infimum. Zorn's lemma gives a minimal admissible $p$.

First put $r(t)=\inf_{n\ge1}p(t^n)^{1/n}$. In the power of a finite cover, commutativity groups words with the same generator counts. The multiplicative constants in exponential bounds for each generator can therefore be paid once per generator, independently of the power. Taking roots gives the cover inequality for $r$; repeated use of a kept triple gives its analogue for $r$. Minimality forces $r=p$ and hence $p(t^n)=p(t)^n$. For each factor $f$ with $p(f)>0$, use the localized gauge
\[
 q_f(t)=\inf_{n\ge0}\frac{p(tf^n)}{p(f)^n}.
\]
Power equality gives $q_f(1)=1$. Localization preserves the triples and $q_f\le p$, so again $q_f=p$. Shifting the infimum by one yields $p(t)p(f)\le p(tf)$, which combines with submultiplicativity for equality. A zero factor follows directly from nonnegativity and the upper bound. Every localization compared itself with the same minimal $p$.

\subhead{Why testing the multiplier matters}
On the additive monoid $\mathbb N^2$, set $p(a,b)=\max(2^a,2^b)$ and use the cover relation that the family contains the element it covers. For $u=(1,0)$ and $v=(0,1)$, $p(u)=p(v)=2$ at the unit. At multiplier $u$ the two compared values are $p(2u)=4$ and $p(u+v)=2$. A.5 requires the comparison at every multiplier.

In E.8, A.7 is applied to the product of all old wires and one designated unit tensor. Both factors have positive values, each can only decrease, and their product is preserved. This fixes both values in one extension and supplies the original wire distances and unit exponent needed by the flat-branch estimate. The selection proof is classical and existential.

\heading{11C. Boundary words preserve the weighted sum}
Fix J.2's boundary states, routing data, valid packet counts and one bounded good outcome. Each network node contributes a local weight in a commutative ring. Each current packet imposes agreement between its two holders. A packet consumed in an earlier move can still occur in a local table. Its copies are then controlled by that table.

\subhead{Regrouping the finite sum}
Partition the nodes into pieces. Let $B$ consist of current packet IDs whose two holders lie in different pieces. An admissible global assignment determines one boundary word $b$ on $B$ and an assignment on each piece. Conversely, assignments on the pieces that agree with $b$ recover the global assignment by restriction to the group containing each node. This is a bijection. Each node occurs exactly once in the product, so the grouped value is
\[
 \sum_{b\ {\rm on}\ B}\ \prod_j
   \left(\sum_{\text{assignments on piece }j\text{ consistent with }b}
         \ \prod_{p\text{ in piece }j}\phi_p\right).
\]
The word indexes individual packets, including parallel packets between the same pair of nodes. In an exact control with two such packets, the left weights for $01,10$ were $2,-1$ and the right weights were $3,5$. Gluing by ID gives $2\cdot3-1\cdot5=1$. Independent choices on the two sides give $(2-1)(3+5)=8$. Identifying the two packet coordinates loses both admissible terms.

\subhead{Where the boundary-size bound enters}
The identity holds for any partition. The useful partition has heavy singleton pieces and full subtrees rooted at light children. For each nonempty piece, all its crossing tree edges touch a single node. H.2 places each current nonexceptional packet between distinct nodes on a parent--child edge. Thus the cut packet IDs for the piece can all be charged to that common node's incident load, plus a separate allowance for exceptional IDs. Two adjacent interior nodes of a four-node path form a connected piece with two disjoint boundary edges, illustrating the role of the stronger shape constraint.

With the parameters of J.2, the incident boundary consumes at most $KT\Lambda/v^{300}$ bits per piece. The $KT\Lambda/k$ total-bit estimate also charges seen copies, move slots and original local work. These statements concern a bounded good outcome after H.2's law expansion. The equality subsequently used in J.3 sums over those outcomes. The contracting laws, their preservation of the weighted network value, and the full machine cost of the dealer remain substantive dependencies.

\heading{11D. Field constants descend together}
Fix a characteristic $p$, an algebraically closed field $K$ of that characteristic and an arbitrary field $F$ of the same characteristic. Suppose a circuit of size $s$ over $F$ computes the image of an integer polynomial. M.1 produces a circuit of the same size over $K$ computing the image of the same polynomial.

\subhead{Why specialization preserves the gate list}
Put the fields in the common nonzero ring $T=F\otimes_k K$, with $k$ the prime field. Once the circuit is fixed, its gate list uses only finitely many constants. In $T$ those constants and $K$ generate a finitely generated $K$-algebra $A$. A presentation of $A$ as a quotient of a polynomial ring has proper kernel. The weak Nullstellensatz gives a $K$-point of this presentation and therefore a homomorphism $A\to K$. The homomorphism preserves all the relations among the finitely many constants at once.

Lift the gates to $A[X]$ following their existing references. Inclusion into $T[X]$ is injective, so the lifted output is the same integer target. Apply $A\to K$ to each gate. Constants specialize, predecessor indices stay available, and the output identity survives. The choice of homomorphism may depend on the candidate circuit. The size bound transfers because this argument applies to each candidate.

A target field containing the required algebraic solutions matters. An element of $\mathbb F_4$ outside $\mathbb F_2$ obeys $z^2+z+1=0$. Neither element of $\mathbb F_2$ satisfies the relation. Its algebraic closure contains a root. This tests the proposed specialization of the constants in the given circuit.

\subhead{Why the exponent is absolute}
For each characteristic the proof chooses an algebraic closure of its prime field. In M.3 the coefficient $c=1/1600^2$ is fixed. With $L=\log_2m$, the contracting branch gives an exponent of the form $a_p L^2/\log_2 L$ in base two, where $a_p>0$. Relative to $cL^{4/3}$ the ratio is
\[
 \frac{a_p}{c}\,\frac{L^{2/3}}{\log_2 L}\longrightarrow\infty .
\]
This supplies a threshold for each fixed characteristic. Only after choosing that threshold does M.3 quantify over fields of that characteristic and their circuits. HC is covered in characteristic two as well; the permanent clause requires $p\ne2$. The exact HC projection in M.2 and the branch lower bounds are additional premises of this conclusion.
