# W04-B • What battery compression can certify

A reduced solution can certify a full-model solution when the projection and reconstruction obey explicit objective inequalities and the reconstructed schedule satisfies the original constraints. The mathematical content lies in establishing these properties for a particular model. Agreement on training schedules, or agreement of two reported objective values, does not establish them. The W03 ideal-storage construction supplies one nontrivial instance.

## Exact thresholds and attainment

Let X and Y be feasible point types. A full point may include a labelled trajectory, its commitment history and any state needed by the constraints. Let J and K be real objectives on X and Y. Define A(J,t) by existence of an x in X with J(x)≤t. Suppose there are maps p:X→Y and f:Y→X with K(p(x))≤J(x) and J(f(y))≤K(y). For each threshold t, p transports a full witness to a reduced witness and f transports a reduced witness back. Thus A(J,t) iff A(K,t) for every real t. Empty types require no special value convention. If both domains are empty both propositions are false. If only one domain is empty the map from the nonempty domain cannot exist.

The proof never inverts an arbitrary labelled trajectory. The interface identity p(f(y))=y, dispatch equivalence, uniqueness and trajectory preservation are separate specifications. In the storage instance the balanced construction does reproduce the returned aggregate path. In the generic cost theorem feasibility and cost are sufficient. A deployment whose reduced variables promise a particular service must include that promise in its lift relation or in the checked constraints.

For the weaker attained-optimum contract take one reduced minimizer ŷ and one full feasible candidate x with J(x)≤K(ŷ). For all u in X, K(ŷ)≤K(p(u))≤J(u), so J(x)=K(ŷ) and x minimizes J. No feasible reconstruction is needed for other reduced points. In a two-valued reduced interface where both states cost 0, a single feasible full state projecting to false also costs 0. The true reduced minimizer has no same-interface realization. With identity reconstruction on a Boolean ambient space, it can land outside the full feasible subset {false}. A separate missing-lift example gives reduced optimum 0 and full optimum 1. Equal infima alone do not even determine endpoint attainment. The sets (0,∞) and [0,∞), with identity cost, both have infimum zero. Only the latter reaches the zero threshold. Its optimizer 0 admits no full point of cost at most 0 under any reconstruction. Conversely, if both optima are attained and equal and only cost is prescribed, a constant map to any full minimizer satisfies the cost test for every reduced minimizer. Failure of reconstruction in that setting must refer to a supplied algorithm or a stronger interface, as in the finite Boolean example.

## Approximation and conditional certificates

Write j=inf J(X), k=inf K(Y). Take nonempty feasible types with both cost ranges bounded below when using real infima. Suppose K(p(x))≤J(x)+α and J(f(y))≤K(y)+β uniformly. For every x, k−α≤J(x), hence k−α≤j. Reversing the direction gives j≤k+β. For a returned ŷ with K(ŷ)≤k+ε and a feasible x̂ with J(x̂)≤K(ŷ)+β, the full regret lies between zero and α+β+ε. This candidate result only requires a certificate on ŷ plus global projection and a reduced suboptimality certificate. A failed or unavailable lift supplies no candidate. For example, full cost is always 1 and reduced costs are (0,1). Projection to the second state has zero distortion. The checked lift of that second state has zero cost error, yet j=1 exceeds k=0. The global upper sandwich for the whole reduced model needs an additional argument. All terms are absolute costs in the same units. A certified reduced lower bound may replace k in a stopping test. A restricted-model dual bound generally fails as a lower bound on j.

Given a validated feasible cost U and valid unrestricted lower bounds L₁,L₂, their maximum L is still a lower bound. Then 0≤U−j≤U−L. Stop when U−L≤ε, or return the lowest-cost retained full-feasible point with its achieved gap. For nested restrictions, a feasible threshold witness at stage r is still feasible at each later stage. The final stage with feasible set X has exactly the original thresholds. Even an empty initial restriction satisfies containment. A bounded-time solver can return a costlier candidate from a larger set. The retain function accepts a trial only if its full cost improves, otherwise keeping the previous full-feasible point. This assumes that one validated incumbent or fallback already exists. No deadline guarantee follows. A single binary variable with cost z and constraint z≥1/2 has integer optimum 1 and relaxed optimum 1/2 even with every variable explicit.

## What has an engineering witness?

For N>0 homogeneous ideal stores with natural-number states, common capacity C and symmetric one-step rate R, the W03 reconstruction splits total S by quotient and remainder. The same device labels receive the remainder at every time. Endpoints use that assignment. The external feasibility predicate and fee depend only on the aggregate path. The throughput charge is k times device movement with k≥0. Projection cannot increase that charge by the triangle inequality. The balanced reconstruction restores the aggregate exactly and its individual changes never cancel. Its full cost equals aggregate cost. StorageContract.lean uses these results through typed feasible projections and lifts.

A proposed action can be replayed against labelled capacity, rate, efficiency, terminal, mode, network and history constraints whenever these are evaluable and the requisite inputs and history are known. Such a replay can check full feasibility and cost at that point. It cannot establish a globally small α from a panel. Uniform projection distortion, global model-valid lower bounds and a causal uncertainty model are independent obligations. The proof uses mathematical real comparisons. Validated numerical tolerances, outward-rounded cost and residual bounds, temporal consistency, emergency fallback availability and machine-to-plant implementation are outside its scope.

W03's heterogeneous fixed-share negative controls use positive device weights. Its terminal residual estimate requires positive discharge efficiencies, the stated state equations and terminal tolerances, and its exact obstruction assumes unequal round-trip efficiencies and exact cyclic endpoints. For weights 1/2, efficiency products 4/5 and 81/100 and discharge efficiencies 1 and 9/10, total charge 1 and discharge 4/5 yield terminal residuals 0 and 1/180. Positive tolerance permits nonzero throughput. The independent rational replay also constructs a two-hour physical witness, charging each battery by 1/2 in hour one and discharging each by 2/5 in hour two. Both start at 1, satisfy capacity 2 and power limit 1, and respect individual mode exclusivity. Their respective terminal values are 1 and 181/180.

## Relation to prior work

Givan, Dean and Greig develop stochastic bisimulation for finite Markov decision processes. Their model equivalences preserve transition and reward information sufficient for value and policy statements. Our deterministic comparison of feasible trajectory objective sublevels neither constructs a Markov quotient nor guarantees online policy transfer. [Equivalence notions and model minimization in Markov decision processes](https://cs.brown.edu/people/tdean/publications/archive/GivanetalAIJ-03.pdf)

Cousot and Cousot relate concrete and abstract program semantics through ordered domains and fixpoint approximation. Sound abstraction and sound concretization are longstanding proof patterns. An inner feasible subset gives incumbents when reconstruction is checked. An outer relaxation may give lower bounds in minimization if it contains every full feasible image and its objective is a relaxation. The present inequality contracts do not assert a Galois connection. [Abstract interpretation](https://www.di.ens.fr/~cousot/COUSOTpapers/POPL77.shtml)

Girard and Pappas develop approximate simulation and bisimulation metrics for discrete and continuous systems. Transition matching and output discrepancies are stronger structure than two scalar cost inequalities. Finite-horizon error propagation requires a corresponding transition or action coupling, cost regularity and terminal conditions. None is implied by a feature distance on batteries. [Approximation metrics for discrete and continuous systems](https://georgejpappas.org/papers/J29.pdf)

Öztürk and colleagues study tractable inner approximations to Minkowski sums of distributed flexibility sets and disaggregation. Feasible disaggregation and approximation error depend on device descriptions and construction. This directly motivates checking actual labelled lifts. [Alleviating the curse of dimensionality in Minkowski sum approximations of storage flexibility](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01614)

Dantzig and Wolfe exploit block structure through a master problem and subproblems. The present value inequalities supply neither a pricing oracle nor a convergence rate. Morales-España and Tejada-Arango show how clustered unit commitment can overestimate flexibility when individual transition and ramp constraints disappear. A full-system commitment recovery needs those constraints and the relevant history. [Decomposition principle for linear programs](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.8.1.101) • [Modelling the hidden flexibility of clustered unit commitment](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02622)

The v0.3 manuscript and local VP_VNP_Review are internal research inputs. The externally authored VP/VNP release's statement/proof split and axiom inspection motivated the audit format. None of its lemmas enters the Lean dependency graph here. These optimization inequalities are elementary. The contribution in this packet is the checked specification, its W03 instantiation, explicit failure witnesses and reproducible scope. There is no grid speed, global AC-OPF, SCUC, economic savings or empirical approximation theorem in W04-B.
