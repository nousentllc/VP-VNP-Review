# W06-A · Opportunity screening in deterministic storage scheduling

This directory contains a frozen cross of six new seed blocks, two horizon lengths and six operating regimes. The 72 equal-weight study paths and eight constructed controls are generated locally. The code uses Python 3.12.9, NumPy 2.2.4 and SciPy 1.18.0 with its bundled HiGHS 1.12.0. The archived checker uses the Python standard library. See [findings](FINDINGS.md), [case-level bounds](analysis/cases.csv) and [claim register](claims.csv).

## Contract and units

Each one-hour command a is a signed integer. A positive command c consumes 1.25c MWh AC to add c MWh to inventory. A negative command with magnitude d withdraws d MWh from inventory and supplies d MWh AC. Thus c and d are mutually exclusive, with an 80% round-trip efficiency. The model constrains ramp of the signed storage-side command, SOC, grid import, exogenous power availability, hourly inventory requirements and prescribed actions in the forced-opposition family. There is no export. Demand is continuously available throughout the hour. Metered service and grid import are integer multiples of 0.25 MWh. No transients, network power flow or stochastic observations are modeled.

The native service objective is unmet quarter-MWh. Native cost is quarter-dollars. In native units, grid import g = delivered + 5c − 4d, and the monetary objective is sum(price × g + wear × (5c+4d)) − 4 × credit × (closing SOC − initial SOC). Price and wear are dollars per MWh. Even-indexed seeds enforce identical initial and final SOC. Odd-indexed seeds permit a closing band and use a $35/MWh linear credit. Causal information disadvantages cannot be inferred from deterministic plans fully known at issuance.

The primary feasible comparator starts from idle or mandatory commands and uses two four-hour window sweeps. Each subproblem minimizes unmet service then cost lexicographically, matches the surrounding SOC and ramp and enforces the terminal contract. Its cost comparison holds hourly service to the full original baseline profile. The alternatives see the same full issued schedule and enforce the same contracts. The direct native MILP and whole-horizon exact dynamic program are strong offline planning references. Expensive repairs of missing baselines receive no hypothetical objective. One known case with a deliberately absent baseline and one with a physically rejected baseline remain in each 72-path denominator.

## Certificate chain

1. **Power boxes.** Service shortfall for hour t is at least max(0, demand − grid cap − 4 discharge cap), in native units. For the fixed service cost comparison, each hour's grid import lies in [max(0, delivered − 4 discharge cap), min(grid cap, delivered + 5 charge cap)]. Minimize the tariff term over that box and take the most favorable terminal credit, dropping nonnegative wear. Negative tariffs select maximum imports.
2. **Energy and ramps.** Propagate maximum positive and negative signed commands from zero by at most the ramp magnitude per hour. On an interval, total served cannot exceed cumulative import capacity plus four times the largest permissible decline in SOC. Charging losses are nonnegative. Initial stock and terminal recoverability tighten the endpoint envelopes. A prefix recurrence sums disjoint interval shortfalls and valid hourly shortfalls. For fixed-service cost, enumerate allowed integer closing SOC s. Total imports exceed or equal total delivered plus 4(s−s0)+max(0,s−s0). For each possible s, minimize tariff over ramp-limited import boxes and this aggregate floor by filling cheaper hours first. Add the minimum wear implied by s−s0 and its exact credit and then minimize across attainable or relaxed closing stocks. Reuse the maximum of all applicable lower bounds.
3. **Full continuous relaxation.** Relax metering, integer commands and converter-mode integrality. Retain the original force/ramp/state/grid constraints, the converter big-M rows, the charge/discharge boxes and closing obligations. For objective c'x+K with Ax=b, Gx≤h and finite l≤x≤u, let y be free and m≤0. With r=c−A'y−G'm, K+b'y+h'm+sum min(r_j l_j,r_j u_j) is a valid lower bound for any such y,m. The experiment converts floating HiGHS marginals into rational multipliers with bounded denominators, projects inequality signs and recomputes every dot product exactly. check.py reconstructs the coefficients independently as six scalar expressions per hour. Solver primal and dual tolerances never supply the proof. The bound is an application of weak duality. [Boyd and Vandenberghe, chapter 5](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf) · [SciPy linprog](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.linprog.html)
4. **Integer objective.** Every feasible monetary value in native units is divisible by the common divisor of the price, throughput and terminal-credit coefficients. Service is integral. Round the best lower bound upward onto this lattice. This addition was registered before holdout generation. [Pre-execution note](protocol/lattice_note.md)
5. **Exact discrete optimum.** Forward DP keeps the best partial schedule per (SOC, previous command). Every feasible command is enumerated and exact integer costs select the best endpoint meeting the contract. For service, maximizing the current delivered energy conditional on command is valid because delivery leaves subsequent requirements unchanged and the primary objective rewards every delivered unit. For the economic contract delivery is fixed externally. A separate backward recurrence enumerates attainable next SOC and predecessor ramps, reconstructs stage costs independently and returns the minimum at the required initial state. The opposite traversal and independent MILP encoding distinguish model implementation errors from solver arithmetic. [SciPy milp](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.milp.html)
