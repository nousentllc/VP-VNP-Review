# W06-E findings

**Original W05 economics.** All 252 historical 24-hour fill trajectories and 504 economic LP gap fractions reproduced. Every recorded fill fully attains the mandatory cumulative AC discharge lower bound. With fixed hourly service, fixed end stock, flat $50/MWh energy, $10/MWh throughput and $50/MWh terminal credit, exactly zero cost improvement is possible on these cases. This analytic strengthening was motivated by the small pre-existing LP residuals. For cumulative served energy Y, AC discharge D, stock change ΔS, charge efficiency η and compiled reciprocal discharge efficiency b, exact cost is pY−vS₀+[(p+w)/η−v]ΔS+[(p+w)b/η+w−p]D. Both nonconstant coefficients are positive. The import envelope considers feeder power, available charging, and bidirectional ramp propagation. D is at least the sum of positive hourly served load minus this envelope. The exact fill reaches that sum; thus the entire remaining opportunity is bounded by the storage coefficient times end inventory above the legal floor. A two-period example in controls.py saves $90 at fixed service and stock under variable prices, and fails the flat-tariff premise.

**Continuous W05-scale stress test.** Splicing original-scale days without resetting grid ramp, SOC or hourly floors shows a maximum $1,175.53 ceiling per entire horizon for these paths (rounding of the upper bound to cents). Twelve paths passed. Four 30-day paths yielded $39.18/day, and eight seven-day paths $89.66–$167.93/day. The historic independently initialized panel means were $990.79 and $760.58/day. These splices are an intervention on archived synthetic inputs. They are unsuitable for estimating real monthly performance.

**New duty cycle.** Eight independent seed families drove paired 7/30-day simulations on a different 12 MWh synthetic unit. Total path hours were 7,104. Pending jobs crossed all 280 internal midnights; state, previous setpoint and signed reserve capacity persisted. At equal hourly service and common ending inventory, perfect-foresight headroom against the causal price rule averaged $100.91/$83.18 per site-day. The gate permitted a new controller. The exactly solved finite-state plant assumes discrete actuation. Release audit confirmed 8,260,320 Bellman edge evaluations across 128 exact contracts, plus feasible extremal paths.

**Terminal-only economics.** On identical realized hourly service at a flat tariff, the difference of exact offline optimal costs between the matched-fill end inventory and floor-plus-credit contracts was $4.21/$0.67 per day. Independent daily reinitialization on the same hourly demands yielded $28.43/$26.63 per day. Under the variable-price/optional reserve contract, the continuous stock-only effect was $25.39/$7.35 per day. With credit $0 all released-stock optimum paths end at 2 MWh. With $100 the range is 4–11. These are conditional bounds or exact offline optimum differences, never an online revenue claim.

**Controller experiment.** The development-fitted H12 marginal mark of $65/MWh improved on the price-aware $70 single-step rule by $55.32/$40.45 per site-day, and on H12 zero-tail optimization by $46.96/$48.40. Each contrast exceeded $15 in eight of eight seeds; the four Holm-adjusted sign-test p-values were .015625. Holding the H12 forecast information constant, H6 no-tail decision costs were further from the full-information optimum. Eight seeds limit inference; both horizons share seed families. Every applied primary action passed service, plant, ramp, reserve and terminal requirements. The guard rejected 83 first actions. Timing across the six primary/diagnostic streams counted 42,624 decisions, 0 timed misses, and 232.42 ms maximum on this machine. Complete clocks including development and certificates are in the gate, comparison and audit files. Dollars here cover the private modeled cost function. Incremental deployment costs remain unknown.

**Falsification by stronger policies.** Against a simple untuned $50 H12 terminal mark, the candidate gained $38.49/$39.14 per day. A three-scenario stochastic H12 controller sharing the $65 mark and issued information had a lower mean cost than the candidate by $2.03/$2.35 per site-day. Zero trajectories exceeded the candidate's $15 target over this comparator. These post-primary checks qualify the preregistered result and argue against generalized controller superiority. An explicit HiGHS time-expanded flow LP reproduced the rolling DP objectives and admitted each recorded first action for all 24 declared test times.

**Service-value falsifier.** A separate full-information arm could discard low-tier jobs for $18 each. The objective fell by $327.79/$328.03 per day, but 980/1,253 and 4,278/5,558 low jobs were lost across the respective panels. This is a failure of matched service even though critical load was protected. The fixed-service policies lose none. Jobs are booked-slot energy batches rather than freely shiftable ML workloads. The model omits reserve deployment, true aging, queueing, telemetry, forecasting from market data, hardware-in-loop evaluation and calendar continuity between separate seed families.

**Decision.** Retire conversion of isolated-day terminal inventory ceilings into recurring daily revenue. Advance conditional flat-price discharge certificates and terminal-aware stochastic benchmarks on a fresh, higher-resolution generator with calibrated prices, wear, service risk and tighter physical constraints. Keep the scenario comparator as the reference. No deployment or net revenue claim passes.
