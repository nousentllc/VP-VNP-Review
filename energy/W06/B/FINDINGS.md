## What can justify stopping

For any candidate cost j above a valid floor L, a reference cost U gives gain U−j ≤ U−L. Pointwise validity does not require existence of an optimizer. Baseline membership and a uniform lower bound imply nonempty and bounded-below objective values. Only then do we use the real infimum for a nonnegative optimality gap. The maximization theorem uses a uniform upper bound and supremum. Empty domains receive no numerical optimum in this application. A solver run that returns a numerical lower bound does not supply a feasible baseline. Small equality residuals alone do not establish a feasible upper witness. A feasible alternative supplies a lower witness on the best achievable improvement in its own policy class, including history, deadline and reliability obligations.

A ceiling strictly below ε excludes gain ≥ε. A ceiling ≤ε excludes gains >ε. At equality, a feasible point may achieve ε exactly. A separate proof that the certified floor cannot be attained *does* exclude gain ≥ε at equality. For example, take admissible costs x>0, baseline 1, and lower floor zero. The infimum is zero but every gain is strictly below one. The practical exclusion is per candidate, with no uniform strictly smaller real upper bound in that example.

A priced cost floor K in the same declared units and horizon bounds net benefit by U−L−K. The inequality only uses K ≤ the actual incremental cost. Interpreting K as an implementation cost floor additionally requires adequate attribution and accounting. The Lean input type carries unit and horizon tags. The type checker rejects accidental mixing of two tags. A user can still mislabel input numbers. Discounted period bounds allow arbitrary nonnegative discount weights and valid per-period floors along each feasible path.

## Finite scenarios, information and risk

Nonnegative weights preserve the sum of the per-case inequalities. Weights need sum to one when interpreting the sum as a mean. The theorem also covers unnormalized weights, e.g. discount factors. Safety on a partial panel requires a valid fallback floor for each missing scenario at its original weight. Renormalizing or dropping failed cases can change which targets appear excluded. Same-horizon scenario paths need not be independent. A finite deterministic sum gives no external prevalence or population confidence guarantee.

For one common objective, the checked chain successively imposes physical feasibility, history compatibility and a deadline predicate. A candidate is adapted when indistinguishable histories induce the same action. Refining the observation partition preserves implementability of any policy that used only the old observations. This is a finite comparison of specified classes. Mapping two different grids, horizons, objective functions or contract definitions requires an explicit feasible mapping and objective domination. Tightening a floor affects its informativeness. It cannot on its own demonstrate that a policy attains the new headroom.

The separable floor can substantially overstate improvement available online. Consider two equally likely, indistinguishable future states, a common initial action x in [0,1], and respective losses x and 1−x. A clairvoyant optimizer takes different endpoint actions and reaches zero loss in each state. Every admissible shared action costs exactly one half on average, including the baseline. Add penalties 1/2−x and x−1/2 in the respective states. For any admissible action the weighted penalty is zero and each modified state cost is one half. The new certificate closes the entire apparent gap. In general, if every admissible common policy has nonpositive expected penalty and L_s bounds its augmented scenario cost from below, the weighted floors bound the common-policy cost. This is a finite weak-duality instance of Brown, Smith and Sun. Its use on W06 engineering cases would require auditable policy-wide centering, valid augmented floors and a measured computational budget. [Prior art](LITERATURE.md)

To certify cost, constrain service, risk and stock in the actual candidate set before taking a floor. Equal service can coexist with arbitrarily large cost opportunity. In a two-point equiprobable loss panel, moving from (50,50) to (0,80) improves mean cost from 50 to 40 and worsens upper-half CVaR from 50 to 80. A distinct sufficient condition uses pointwise rather than mean control. If every baseline loss exceeds its corresponding lower floor by at most e, and the risk functional is monotone and translates an added constant e into an e shift, then baseline risk minus candidate risk is ≤e. The theorem assumes those two properties for the selected loss functional.

## Numerical certificates

For Ex=d and Ax≤b choose any y and z≤0. With r=c−Eᵀy−Aᵀz, the exact LP bound is dᵀy+bᵀz+Σ min(rⱼℓⱼ,rⱼuⱼ). The Lean proof expands both transposed matrix products and handles the two residual signs. It requires each original candidate trajectory to lie in the relaxation and to have the same cost or a certified objective-transfer allowance. Real feasibility of the witness enforces consistency of each lower and upper endpoint pair.

The mixed-row formulation treats both row activities and variables as intervals. A lower-only interval has a finite floor for nonnegative residual, an upper-only interval for nonpositive residual, and a free interval for zero residual. A finite closed interval permits either sign, including singleton equality rows. At incompatible signs the checker returns no finite component certificate. Coupling or another multiplier may still prove a finite bound on the original model. These conclusions need no optimal multiplier, solver dual-feasibility flag, strong duality or finite boxes on every column. A certified uncertainty allowance on objective transfer adds to the ceiling. Constraint-coefficient uncertainty additionally needs a feasible embedding.

Independent Python rational controls checked 2,250 feasible boxed model/multiplier combinations with 120 tight at the tested witness, 12 interval/sign configurations and 1,071 common-action/distribution points. These are exhaustive grids of specified finite controls, without population sampling. A decimal displayed as 0.1 differs from its stored binary64 dyadic rational. Use the declared coefficient representation and a checked feasible model/primal when invoking a numerical certificate. The Lean theorem does not parse or validate the campaign solvers' models. ## Chronological terminal inventory

With one constant per-MWh inventory credit, the sum of successive changes in inventory valuation equals the endpoint difference. Two schedules starting at the same SOC and ending inside [ℓ,u] differ by at most v(u−ℓ) in the terminal *valuation component*. Dividing by the number of consecutive days amortizes this component. Total dispatch cost can also change. If every feasible relaxed-terminal schedule has a feasible exact-terminal schedule obeying all other requirements, with extra total cost ≤K, then 0 ≤ inf J_exact − inf J_floor ≤K. No optimum must be attained. The premise is a uniform feasible repair or completion certificate. Without it, even one additional final MWh can require arbitrarily expensive replacement energy or be physically unavailable. Neither SOC width nor a declared accounting credit automatically sets K. This result was pursued separately from the initial B plan and independently of E outcomes. [Rationale](extension_plan.md) · [Proof](formal/Closing.lean)

## Disposition

Advance interval-checked screening with explicit contract and cost units, full panel coverage and an auditable upper witness. Test common-information penalties only where perfect-foresight gaps survive cheaper floors. Document model validation and price/actuation uncertainty before any field stop decision. Retire any inference from a positive ceiling to deployable savings, from mean gains to tail reliability, or from one-time closing credit to long-run cost savings. Open obligations include a verified serializer/certificate checker for the actual large numerical instances, population sampling or robust ambiguity sets, certified end-state repairs on real contracts, and any independent formal kernel/toolchain replication.

## Post hoc flat-tariff addendum

E's historical W05 analysis suggested a stronger result than a small numerical LP gap. At fixed hourly service under a flat tariff, unit prices let the cost decompose into two sufficient quantities, final SOC and total discharge. The archived fill schedule exactly attains a valid discharge lower bound on every one of 252 original days and 12 historical splices. Equal final stock consequently gives **exactly zero** remaining modeled cost opportunity on every matched path. W06-B reproduced this with an all-pairs ramp envelope and an independently expressed exact plant/feasibility checker. The model theorem and 10,272 hourly transitions are documented in [the addendum](flat_review/README.md). Across the separately audited core and addendum, 61 named propositions were checked, five positive theorem modules received same-kernel replay, and eleven intended false statements or type mismatches were rejected. The original 49-declaration proof record remains in results/lean.
