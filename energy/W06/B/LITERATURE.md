# Prior art and attribution

The mathematical results in this track specialize established bounds to a declared engineering contract. The contribution evaluated here is an auditable decision procedure and its controls. Priority or novelty of the underlying inequalities has not been established.

| Primary or author source | Specific relationship |
|---|---|
| Boyd & Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization* (2004), §§5.2.2 and 5.5.1. [Author textbook](https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf) | Weak duality and a feasible-point suboptimality certificate give the incumbent-minus-bound interval. They support stopping when a target falls beyond the certified interval. W06 spells out the equality case for a practical improvement threshold. |
| Land & Doig, *An Automatic Method of Solving Discrete Programming Problems* (1960), *Econometrica* 28, 497–520. [Original paper](https://jmvidal.cse.sc.edu/library/land60a.pdf) | Search compares bounds on remaining subproblems with a feasible incumbent. This is a predecessor of the research stopping rule and of ordinary MIP gap reporting. The energy opportunities require independently validated feasibility and a bound for the same objective. |
| Neumaier & Shcherbina, *Safe bounds in linear and mixed-integer linear programming* (2004). [Authors’ preprint](https://optimization-online.org/2002/06/494/) | Rigorous postprocessing of approximate duals and rigorous bounds on the variables precede W06. Our matrix theorem handles a specific exact support calculation and tests absent endpoints. It does not verify a branch-and-cut solver or its presolve. |
| Birge & Louveaux, *Introduction to Stochastic Programming*, 2nd ed. (2011), chapter “The Value of Information and the Stochastic Solution,” pp. 163–177. [Publisher chapter listing](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4614-0237-4) | The wait-and-see and common-policy comparison is a classical value-of-information distinction. W06 additionally indexes the action class by an explicit deadline predicate. |
| Brown, Smith & Sun, *Information Relaxations and Duality in Stochastic Dynamic Programs* (2010), *Operations Research* 58, 785–801, §2, weak-duality lemma. [Authors’ paper](https://people.duke.edu/~psun/bio/opre.1090.0796v1.pdf) | Their information relaxation and penalty construction is substantially more general than our finite-panel weak bound. Sign conventions here concern minimization, with additive penalties having nonpositive weighted mean for each admissible policy. The two-scenario example isolates perfect-foresight optimism. |
| Horvitz, *Reasoning about Beliefs and Actions under Computational Resource Constraints* (1987), UAI, 429–444. [Author record and paper](https://www.erichorvitz.com/u87.htm) | Additional computation consumes resources and time. A measured fixed implementation-cost floor over a matched horizon can justify a stop. A loose bound alone supplies little evidence to invest. Estimating expected value of a particular computation requires more inputs. |
| Rockafellar & Uryasev, *Optimization of Conditional Value-at-Risk* (2000), *Journal of Risk* 2, 21–41. [Authors’ paper](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~rtr/papers/rtr179-CVaR1.pdf) | This gives a separate tail-loss objective. For two equiprobable atoms the upper-half loss average equals their maximum. The example with a better mean and worse upper tail disproves inferring risk improvement from mean gain. |

The W05 review supplies the historical boxed objective and sign convention. W06-B checks an abstract dependency-free statement with respect to the W05 model code. All Lean modules still share Lean/mathlib dependencies. The W06 numerical studies and these mathematical proofs share a specification and should not count as separate empirical replications.
