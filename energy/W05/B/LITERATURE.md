# W05-B · Primary literature and relationship to the proposition

**Parametric clipping.** Cyrus and Beck derive an intersection algorithm for a line and a convex polygon or polyhedron. Substituting a one-dimensional path into each supporting half-space gives a scalar interval, whose intersection gives the allowable part of the line. W05 fixes one feasible endpoint of a segment and takes its first feasible point as seen from the candidate. This specialization explains the maximum of per-row ratios. The underlying clipping operation predates these experiments. [Cyrus and Beck, *Generalized two- and three-dimensional clipping* (1978), original article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0097849378900213)

**A close algorithmic precedent.** Tordesillas, How and Hutter identify an affine hull and an interior reference, then clip an input displacement against the boundary along its ray. For polyhedral rows their §IV-A computes normalized row directional derivatives divided by reference slack. In W05 notation let v=x̃−a, outward distance parameter q=1−λ, and residual uᵢ at x̃. A violated row has Aᵢv=sᵢ+uᵢ. Its admissible outward weight is sᵢ/(sᵢ+uᵢ). Taking the smallest such weight gives exactly the W05 segment endpoint when the reference is strictly interior relative to the inequality system. RAYEN constructs the affine hull and interior point offline. W05 permits feasible boundary anchors by treating zero margins explicitly, assesses cost against a separate reduced objective, and supplies a Lean-checked statement in this setting. The engineering study has different models, baselines and clocks. [Tordesillas, How and Hutter, *RAYEN*, 2023 version, §§III and IV-A, Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.08336v1)

**Distance bounds and the anchor map.** Hoffman's main theorem bounds the distance to some feasible solution of a consistent affine inequality system via a positive homogeneous measure of the positive residual. For a Lipschitz cost this can supply an existence bound on the cost difference for a sufficiently close feasible point. It does not select the feasible point obtained by returning along our chosen anchor segment. A boundary anchor can give order-one loss as violation tends to zero even when a different feasible point has vanishing distance. The W05 formula can be evaluated at a supplied point and anchor. The Hoffman estimate and this particular constructed displacement solve different certification problems. [Hoffman, *On Approximate Solutions of Systems of Linear Inequalities* (1952), §2, pp. 263–265](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/049/4/V49.N04.A05.pdf)

**Equality completion.** Penrose's generalized-inverse solvability criterion for AXB=C includes the vector equation as a special case. For a residual equation EΔ=r the necessary quantity is E applied to the chosen correction. W05 verifies ER(d−Ew)=d−Ew directly. Penrose's identity EE⁺r=r on the range gives one way to meet it. A right inverse changes the point and can change every inequality and the objective. The requirement to recheck both remains. DC3 also organizes feasibility into equality completion and inequality correction. Its differentiable gradient corrections are a different algorithm from exact affine segment clipping. [Penrose, *A generalized inverse for matrices* (1955), Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100030401) · [Donti, Rolnick and Kolter, *DC3*, ICLR 2021, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.12225)

**Storage flexibility.** Öztürk, Faulwasser, Worthmann, Preißinger and Rheinberger approximate aggregate Minkowski sums through feasible vertex constructions. Their §VI reconstructs individual actions for points represented by such inner approximations. In an inner set, admissible aggregate points carry a route to device-level schedules. W05 instead supplies conditional recovery from an enlarged slice with a verified anchor and original rows. A relaxed point may have no same-interface physical witness. An engineer promising a particular service profile must therefore preserve or explicitly certify that interface during recovery. [Öztürk et al., *Alleviating the Curse of Dimensionality in Minkowski Sum Approximations of Storage Flexibility* (2023 version), §VI and Algorithm 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.01614v1)

These original articles establish precedents for the geometry, residual bounds, equality completion and flexibility reconstruction. They do not substantiate this campaign's measured timing, β tightness or dispatch behavior. Those are internal research outputs. The contribution supported here is a scoped formal check, a computable projection/lift accounting, explicit failure cases and an engineering experiment on the specified model.
