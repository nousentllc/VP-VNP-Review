# An ex-post upper bound on the value of better service control

**Rationale.** W04-only development selected the rule that fills inventory whenever spare grid and device capacity are available. It was therefore possible that even a controller with perfect forecasts could rarely improve its shedding. The campaign added a post-freeze optimality-gap diagnostic after development. Its output did not influence policy selection, hyperparameters or any online action.

For each 24-hour realized day let U be the exact service-shedding cost of nominal fill and let J* be the minimum possible physically feasible shedding on that day. Relax mode exclusivity to obtain the clairvoyant LP. Use the same delivered load, grid and physical storage limits, reserve/floor calendar and terminal requirement. For equality matrix E, inequality matrix A, right-hand sides d,b, objective c and finite boxes [l,u], choose any equality multipliers y and any inequality multipliers z≤0. Put r=c−Eᵀy−Aᵀz. The scalar

    L = dᵀy + bᵀz + Σ min(r_j l_j, r_j u_j)

is a lower bound on the clairvoyant LP optimum by weak duality. Therefore every feasible controller, including one using future information, can improve on nominal fill by at most U−L in shedding on this stored day. Validity does not rely on solver-reported optimality. SciPy/HiGHS supplied candidate binary64 dual multipliers, converted to exact fractions, with violated multiplier signs projected to zero. All dot products and boxes in the certificate use rational arithmetic on stored coefficients. The independent checker reconstructs every row, boxes, restricted signs, primal action and cost from the forecast file using Python's standard library.

The primal rule itself is evaluated in exact arithmetic, with the reciprocal discharge efficiency treated as exactly its stored binary64 matrix coefficient, and each reconstructed action satisfies power balance, SOC dynamics and bounds, exclusivity, reserve, availability, grid ramp and terminal requirements. The nominal rational rule's action vector differed from its logged floating-point counterpart by at most 5.69e−14 on any component. Upper bounds pertain literally to the rational schedules and matrices. Their numerical agreement with the realized simulated trajectories is separately measured. No energy price, degradation or service-penalty parameter enters this bound.

The main panel's mean is exactly below 1/10 by fraction comparison. Its mean also equals the participating latent-day mean, because every seed contributes three variants with equal weight. Thus the primary 0.1 MWh/day gate was unattainable in the sampled full-information service model against nominal fill, independently of the tested predictor's calibration. The alternative panel leaves more room for improvement on that metric. Neither mean bounds value on an unfamiliar day, a different terminal inventory contract, larger low-load variation, different hardware, monetary cost, or field parameter uncertainty. Four deliberate corruptions of the primal, multiplier sign, claimed U and claimed L were rejected.

The output directory contains standalone exact multiplier and trajectory witnesses with [integer numerator, positive integer denominator] for every rational quantity. Fresh LP multipliers can vary by solver version; the shipped witnesses remain directly checkable.

## Alignment with the live score, end inventory and tolerance

The live comparator had no timeouts or fallback substitutions. Summing its stored binary64 hourly shedding entries as exact rationals gives a maximum day-cost difference from nominal rational fill of 2.66e−14 MWh across both panels. The largest absolute closing-inventory difference was 4.62e−14 MWh. Mean *logged comparator shedding* minus the exact lower bound was 0.044250279782861 MWh/day primary and 0.237859490925079 alternative; the primary comparison with 0.1 is an exact fraction test. The common closing requirement is SOC at least 95 MWh, not an exactly fixed 95 MWh end stock. Rational fill ends between 95 and 180 MWh on both panels, with means 166.64149 and 149.99558 MWh. Its surplus energy is permitted by every service model and valued explicitly in the economics, never credited as flexible load served. Enforcing identical final inventory or optimizing purchased energy is a different comparison. The dominant rule uses the current observation, current reserve/floor and plant state. Giving it the same issue-time forecast as the optimizer does not require it to use the forecast.

As a numerical sensitivity, independently relax every equality's absolute residual allowance, inequality RHS and both finite variable bounds by a common tolerance τ=1e−5 in the indicated row/variable units. The same y,z and r yield the valid lower bound L−τ(Σ|y|+Σ|z|+Σ|r|). The resulting logged-baseline mean possible improvements remain at most 0.04455081 MWh/day on the main panel and 0.23832209 on the alternative. These sensitivity bounds apply to vectors within those specified componentwise tolerances and do not cover unknown physical parameter errors.
