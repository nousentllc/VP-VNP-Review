# W05-E · A stronger control defeats the continuation advantage

**Neither confirmatory benefit passed.** On 60 new latent days, the learned six-hour continuation controller shed an additional 8.054 MWh/day against the best eligible six-hour rule. Its 95% paired day-bootstrap interval was +6.039 to +10.258. At twelve hours the excess was 21.225 MWh/day, interval +18.254 to +24.259, against the development-selected H12-information comparator. H6 had zero improved, 11 tied and 49 worse latent days. H12 had zero improved, zero tied and 60 worse. Positive is worse for shedding. The two one-sided tests used a Bonferroni upper 97.5% bound and required that bound to be below −0.1 MWh/day.

## Why the comparator matters

The W04-development rule competition used the original forecast target, doubling its prospective deficit component, and charging up to the usable capacity whenever incremental import capacity was free of competing current load. Spare-capacity filling won at all horizons with 31.055 MWh/day mean shedding on W04. The previous H6 and H12 rules achieved 53.557 and 35.396. At H12, nested H6 filling tied full-information filling. The fixed order selected rule12. The winner makes its action from the current load, availability, reserve, floor and state, while having the same eligibility to see the full horizon.

The confirmatory comparison is against a controller selected entirely before the new holdout. W04's H6 target still improved on W04's original H6 rule on the new main panel by 4.386 MWh/day (95% interval 2.125 to 6.739 MWh/day of reduction). Our conditioned H6 target also reduced shedding against W04's target by 11.498 MWh/day, a secondary comparison. These improvements did not beat the stronger rule.

## Reliability, latency and physical actions

The complete record contains 5,040 day/policy trajectories and 120,960 hourly actions, including the offline reference. Five deliberately damaged action arrays failed independent physical validation. The largest residual in the original record was 1.98e−13. The runner logged zero missing trajectories, solver errors, hard violations and deadline fallbacks. On the primary comparison H6's upper 97.5% bootstrap bound on extra CVaR90 was +17.415 MWh/day and H12's +28.693. Both exceeded the predeclared +5 MWh/day risk margin, so both failed the combined reliability gate despite successful physical checks. For zero hard failures over 60 independent latent days, the exact one-sided 95% upper bound on the probability of at least one such day is 4.87% under independent-identically-distributed Bernoulli sampling. For 24 days it is 11.73%. The deterministic finite panel supplies no site-level assurance.

The longest recorded candidate clock was 27.18 ms and the longest common-fallback-ready clock was 1.18 ms or less. All 5,040 fresh controlled-clock replays reproduced their original action arrays bit-for-bit, including the reference. The original timeout signals were all false. This is a same-host controlled-decision replay. Per-decision clocks include reading the eligible forecast, verifying the common fallback, testing the candidate and its first-step constraints. They exclude program initialization, bulk input decoding, raw serialization, metering, communication and physical actuation. The original driver did not separately instrument each whole-trajectory wall clock. Its raw data retain every individual decision clock and the offline solve time.

## Eligible information, adaptation and service accounting

The compute-matched adaptive policy solved both windows, as did the fixed-H12 control. It selected the shorter available prefix on 1,405 of 4,320 main-panel hours and reduced shedding by 6.995 MWh/day against the dual-solve fixed-H12 policy. That contrast measures an online policy choice. Its service still trailed the best H12-information comparator. The main-panel issued tail reduced load MAE to 5.896 MW from 18.101 for a flat tail over 16,740 overlapping issue/target entries. Nevertheless, using that issued tail increased learned H12 shedding by 11.005 MWh/day (9.146 to 12.945) versus its constant-tail intervention. In the alternative generator that latter effect changed sign, −0.653 (−4.146 to +2.926). A fixed policy's response to a better forecast does not establish a universal value-of-information result. The nested-short policies and full H6 originals have the identical-input scheduling checks in nested_checks.csv. All controller LPs used the same native engine. These are controller and comparator effects.

The site pays for grid imports and throughput and retains closing inventory. Each simulated hour is one hour long. The economics table varies published-in-protocol price *scenarios*, wear and a daily integration/assurance charge. The hypothetical service beneficiary's value per optional electrical MWh is left as v. For flat $50/MWh imports, $10/MWh of battery throughput, $50/MWh terminal stock, $0 differential fixed integration charge and $0.20/billable single-worker-hour computing cost, learned H6 minus fill has mean net change approximately $1,517.31 − 8.054v/day. Learned H12 minus fill gives $2,597.03 − 21.225v/day. Positive values favor the learned policy for this cost scenario. The H6 and H12 break-even service values are $188.39 and $122.36 per delivered flexible MWh, respectively. Lower energy use partly reflects more curtailment and lower closing inventory. For example main-panel H6 learner averaged 99.44 MWh closing inventory versus 166.64 for fill. Purchased-energy terms already incorporate physical losses. The hardware cannot supply the committed reserve during an outage. Each primary outage variant loses 16 MW·h of reserved headroom (8 MW for two hours). The check permits this known unavailable contract quantity, while still enforcing the remaining device and dispatch requirements. Thus zero simulated dispatch violations does not mean all contracted reserve was available. Economic comparisons of a less reliable policy require acceptance of those reliability differences.

The complete arm table reports served energy, physical losses, imports, wear proxy, peak, terminal and tail measures. Pareto comparisons use lower shedding, lower imports, lower throughput, lower CVaR90 and higher end inventory, all at 1e−6 comparison tolerance. Each alternative set preserves conflicting efficiency and service dimensions. The scenario coefficients, penalty on wear and terminal stock valuation are analytic assumptions. Actual revenue, completed computing jobs, accredited MW and utility performance require information outside these experiments.

## Exploration beyond the protocol

A separate rational upper/lower certificate bounds the service improvement a clairvoyant competitor could obtain over exact nominal fill by at most 0.044251 MWh/day averaged across the main panel. This is smaller than the 0.1 MWh/day practical gate even before restricting the competitor to available forecasts. See EXPLORATORY.md, opportunity_certificates.json.gz and its independent solver-free checker. This is a retrospective opportunity bound with perfect future information. No held-out solver or policy received it.

Independent exact summation of the *actual logged* fill-rule shedding differs from the nominal feasible certificate by at most 2.66e−14 MWh/day. Its primary mean minus the lower bound remains 0.04425028 MWh/day; the upper bound is even below 0.044551 when all offline LP rows and boxes are relaxed componentwise by 1e−5. The site requires a 95 MWh closing floor, while the fill rule is allowed to retain surplus stock. Its closing SOC ranged from 95 to 180 MWh. Equal final state, priced imports or different reserve-delivery obligations define different objectives or feasible sets. The out-of-generator ceiling of 0.23786 exceeds the practical margin.
