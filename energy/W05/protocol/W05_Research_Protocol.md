## W05-A — Certified relaxation screening

**Question:** Does explicit early relaxation screening outperform strong solver-native presolve/heuristics and LP-informed recovery at matched quality and budget? A full LP may be sufficient only if the returned candidate satisfies all original discrete and physical constraints. Independently validate a lower bound for the actual unrestricted model. Distinguish exact rational evaluation for stored coefficients from engineering parameter uncertainty.

Use W04 as development; pin fresh, non-overlapping seeds and stress families including forced opposing directions, negative prices, outage, tight endpoint/ramp/reserve, heterogeneous efficiencies and larger assets/horizons. Include cases with a known feasible fallback and cases without one; do not infer full-model infeasibility from restricted failure. Compare tuned native full MILP, LP-only with common fallback, LP-informed repair/start, and explicit screening plus escalation; match all information, solver tuning budgets, CPU threads and end-to-end deadlines. If possible isolate the host and run a second machine. Include build, presolve, LP solve, validation, certificate evaluation, transfer, recovery and fallback-selection time.

Primary endpoint: fraction of cases meeting U−L ≤ 0.00002 + 0.001 max(1,|U|) by 2 s, all three prespecified rotated repeats. Secondary: independently feasible incumbent quality at 50/100/250/500/1,000/2,000 ms, normalized incumbent cost integral (handle zero fallback-to-bound interval explicitly), first valid action, fallback/timeout rate, and full runtime distribution. Freeze practical threshold (e.g. 0.1% of max(1,|fallback objective|) where fallback exists) and paired inferential procedure on development. Compare screening against both full MILP and LP-informed comparator, not only weaker direct solver. Track the native solver's existing presolve and whether the same LP is duplicated.

**Falsifier:** no material incremental deadline benefit over strong LP repair/native solver; invalid LP acceptances or worse feasible outcomes at the same quality gate; substantial overhead. A high LP-admissibility rate is a descriptive property of the sample, not a population estimate or a new optimization algorithm.

## W05-B — Constructive, computable AIPC error bounds

**Question:** Can we turn α and β in the already established regret bound into useful, instance-specific or uniform engineering certificates? Start from the recorded W04 Lean theorems; do not rename elementary sandwich arguments as novel.

Candidate theorem: in a convex continuous slice B of the original feasible set, impose Ex=d and Ax≤b. Given a feasible anchor x₀ and candidate x̃ satisfying B and Ex=d, let δᵢ=max(0,Aᵢx̃−bᵢ) and σᵢ=bᵢ−Aᵢx₀≥0. For λ=max over violated rows of δᵢ/(δᵢ+σᵢ), with max empty set 0, x⁺=(1−λ)x̃+λx₀ is feasible. A violated row with σᵢ=0 forces λ=1. For affine J, J(x⁺)−J(x̃)=λ[J(x₀)−J(x̃)]. If J is L_J-Lipschitz and ||x̃−x₀||≤D, the nonnegative reconstruction loss is at most L_JDλ. With all violated rows having σᵢ≥σ>0 and δᵢ≤δ̄, this is at most L_JD δ̄/(δ̄+σ). A relaxed candidate objective matched to K gives a calculable lift error β; a separately valid unrestricted L certifies U−J*≤U−L. This is a conditional derivation, not yet Lean-checked or shown original.

Respect exact equalities: a convex mix with a feasible anchor does not repair a nonzero equality residual unless λ=1. Investigate an explicit equality correction (with checked rank/right-inverse and independent constraints), then inequality repair. For MIP, anchor and candidate must lie in a common fixed integral mode slice; never average different binary decisions or call a fractional LP point a feasible lift. Nonconvex AC feasible sets cannot be handled by convex mixing without separate proof. Include terminal SOC, ramp, reserve, availability, loss and couplings in the original model.

Develop a second, storage-specific construction for terminal mismatch: derive correction bounds using available charge/discharge headroom, η_c/η_d, future SOC margins and the grid ramp/import constraints. If no corrective slot exists, prove infeasibility of the proposed lift or return no certificate; distinguish candidate-specific residual repair from a uniform projection bound over all full feasible trajectories. Compare explicit lift to a fixed-mode repair LP and existing inner/outer flexibility approximations. Test heterogeneous losses, tight and slack endpoints, fixed vs opposing modes, zero slack and failed equality conditions.

Formalize the affine repair identity, row-wise feasibility, zero-slack fallback, and any useful domain-specific bound in Lean with pinned dependencies, statement correspondence, exact controls, axiom scope and intentionally false variants. Report bound conservatism relative to realized repair cost and overhead; a loose or expensive certificate can fail the engineering objective even if mathematically correct. If Lean cannot run, supply source and derivation labeled uncompiled. Do not infer a universal bound from finite samples.

## W05-E — Continuation value, prediction horizon and information use (critical)

**Question:** Is it possible to preserve the six-hour continuation benefit while matching or beating the strongest equal-information forecast policy and accounting for energy and wear? W04's H6 result is positive on its generator, but H12 forecast rule beats H12 continuation by 13.08 MWh/day; the best online comparator must not be artificially shortened.

Use W04 as development. Prospectively specify a new sample, e.g. 60+ independent latent days with three paired stress regimes, plus an independent generator or authorized operational forecast vintages if accessible. Freeze issue-time forecasts of up to 12 hours, take common prefixes, and log what each policy can know. Include tail forecast bias, outage/headroom loss, long peaks, correlated errors, ramp limits, terminal recharge and reserve constraints. Keep facility load and optional computing load separate; unserved electricity is a proxy, not jobs completed. Do not manufacture operational traces. Resample at latent-day or appropriate chronological block level, not hourly overlapping forecast errors.

Mandatory comparators: best forecast-aware rule selected on development at each common horizon (3/6/12 h), no-continuation rolling LP, frozen W04 lexicographic precautionary target, and an implementable continuation-value candidate. Compare H6 against equal H6 information and H12 against equal H12; include an H12-information policy that explicitly ignores the tail as a nested policy-class control. Test adaptive horizon as a separate intervention with matched information/compute; selecting the best horizon per realized day is hindsight, not an online policy. Retain offline perfect foresight only as a reference.

Candidate terminal designs: W04 lexicographic target; terminal inventory value or piecewise-linear approximate value function trained only on development; robust reserve/continuation target with forecast-conditioned uncertainty; and optionally constrained multiobjective MPC. Specify priorities and hard reliability rules: a soft penalty must not silently buy a mandatory reserve violation. A horizon that changes forecast information or policy class is a separate treatment. Use deterministic tie-break on first action and explicitly log solver tolerances, timing, fallback selection and delivered simulated action. Check identical-input deterministic replay with controlled deadlines; account for compute and measurement latency separately from physical real-time assurance.

Primary outcome: paired unserved flexible electrical MWh/day at equal hard feasibility and information. Predeclare two confirmatory contrasts (H6 vs strongest H6 rule; H12 or adaptive candidate vs best policy with the same H12 information), practical threshold and multiplicity handling; other horizons/regimes are secondary. Report served energy, imports, terminal SOC, losses, throughput, wear proxy, reserve/headroom availability, worst shortfall, fallback, deadline and risk measures. Require a prespecified noninferiority reliability gate. Report all results, including cases where extra forecast accuracy worsens a fixed policy. Better MAE does not imply Blackwell more informative forecasts or universal negative value of information.

Economic analysis: for ΔS served MWh, price sequence p_t, imports g_t, throughput q_t, marginal wear k_t, terminal inventory difference Δe_T and its agreed shadow value v_T, compute ΔNB = v·ΔS − Σ p_t Δg_t − Σ k_t Δq_t + v_T Δe_T − incremental computing/integration/assurance cost, with units and service beneficiary fixed. Time intervals and weights belong explicitly in sums. If service value is nonuniform or nonlinear, use a stated service-cost function rather than constant v. Losses reflected in purchased energy must not be charged twice. Compare Pareto tradeoffs rather than invent a valuation for computing workload. No realized revenue, accredited MW or national-dollar extrapolation.

**Falsifier:** favorable H6 result disappears against the stronger H6 rule or on independent holdouts, H12/adaptive fails matched strongest comparator, mandatory reliability/fallback deteriorates, or feasible gains do not cover incremental cost under clearly stated scenarios. Distinguish policy improvement from numerical solver improvement.
