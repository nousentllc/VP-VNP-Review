\sect{Findings for the v0.5 review}
The feasible-anchor construction survived mathematical falsification under explicit convexity, equality and feasibility hypotheses. Its strongest limitation was visible on small, fully auditable storage problems. The construction gave feasible dispatches on every qualified case, yet none met the registered near-optimality threshold. A better comparator also changed the predictive-control conclusion: with spare import capacity used to charge the battery, the remaining opportunity for better service was tiny on the primary synthetic panel.

\sect{B \quad When a feasible anchor repairs a candidate}
The manuscript uses AIPC for a refinement strategy that bounds cost changes when projecting to a reduced model and lifting back to the full model. In a convex base set $B$, fix affine equalities $Ex=d$, inequalities $Ax\le b$, a feasible anchor $a$ and a raw candidate $w\in B$ satisfying the equalities. For row $i$ let $\delta_i=(A_iw-b_i)_+$ and $\sigma_i=b_i-A_ia\ge0$. Define
\[
 r_i=\begin{cases}0&\delta_i=0,\\ \delta_i/(\delta_i+\sigma_i)&\delta_i>0,\end{cases}
 \qquad \lambda=\max_i r_i,\qquad x^+=(1-\lambda)w+\lambda a ,
\]
with maximum zero for an empty family. A previously satisfied convex row remains satisfied. In a violated affine row the residual is $(1-\lambda)\delta_i-\lambda\sigma_i$, which is nonpositive exactly when $\lambda\ge r_i$. Thus $\lambda$ is the \emph{smallest} feasible movement fraction on this segment. It need not minimize movement over the whole feasible set. A binding anchor row that the candidate violates forces $\lambda=1$.

For affine $J$, $J(x^+)-J(w)=\lambda[J(a)-J(w)]$. For $L_J$-Lipschitz $J$ the positive part is bounded by $L_J\|w-a\|\lambda$. If all violated rows have slack at least $s>0$ and violation at most $\bar\delta$, the fraction is at most $\bar\delta/(\bar\delta+s)$. When the reduced objective is $K(y)$, charge any discrepancy $J(w)-K(y)$ as well. Pointwise $\beta$ is enough for a returned point given a uniform projection bound and a reduced candidate quality certificate. Uniform $\beta$ needs a bound across all eligible reduced points.

\sub{Equality, integrality and prior results}
If $Ew\ne d$, the mixed residual is $(1-\lambda)(Ew-d)$. Moving a fraction strictly below one cannot repair it. An explicit correction $w'=w+R(d-Ew)$ works on this residual if $ER(d-Ew)=d-Ew$. A full-row-rank $E$ admits a right inverse; for a rank-deficient $E$ the residual already lies in its range because $a$ is feasible. Verify that the chosen correction works on that residual, particularly if corrections have restricted support. The corrected candidate must be rechecked against the base, rows and objective before applying the ray. A common fixed integral mode defines a valid convex slice when the rest of its constraints are convex. Combining different binary modes, or averaging feasible points of nonlinear AC equations, has counterexamples.

Ray--polyhedron clipping follows classical line clipping. For linear inequalities and a strictly interior anchor, \textit{RAYEN} gives the same radial boundary by taking the tightest row along the outward direction. Hoffman bounds distance to \emph{some} feasible point by a residual for a fixed consistent matrix. That result does not bound the cost or distance of this chosen anchor segment. The W05 contributions are statement-level formal checks, boundary-anchor cases, computable distortion accounting and falsification on the specified storage slices. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08336v1}{Tordesillas et al., RAYEN (2023)} \quad\href{https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.049.027}{Hoffman (1952)}.

\sub{Proof boundary}
Lean 4.30.0-rc2 checked 40 new statements against pinned mathlib and the byte-matched W04 AIPC theorem. Seven false variants failed at their intended declarations and have explicit refutations. The statement inventory inspects transitive axioms, confined to \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}. The numerical solver, instrument clocks and field plant sit outside this proof. Exact controls include 3,616 affine-row systems, 21,497 fraction tests, 729 box points and 960 signed single-slot queries. Prebuilt dependency objects and compiler binaries remain trusted.

\sect{B \quad Computing and testing $\alpha$ and $\beta$ on a fleet}
Each case has three heterogeneous devices and eight one-hour intervals. The full fixed-mode model uses nonnegative power magnitudes $z$ in a known sign schedule, with nameplate box $0\le z\le u$. Efficiencies, chronological state of charge, losses, availability, reserve headroom, import limits, ramp and terminal conditions remain individual or coupled as appropriate. Pairs of successive intervals of the \emph{same device} have identical signs and energy coefficients. Write $G$ for within-pair averaging, $S$ for replication and $P=SG$. This reduces 24 continuous decisions to 12. It introduces no equality between the modes of two different batteries.

For a row vector $v$, the box support is $H(v)=\sum_j u_j(v_j)_+$. Set $h_i=H(A_i(P-I))$, $\alpha=H(c^\top(P-I))$. The reduced model evaluates cost $K(y)=c^\top Sy$, replaces the rows by $ASy\le b+h$ and retains the terminal equalities. Since $E(P-I)=0$, every full feasible $z$ projects to $Gz$ at cost at most $c^\top z+\alpha$. For a reduced point, apply the anchor map to $w=Sy$. With $\epsilon=K(y)-L_Y$ for a valid reduced lower bound, and $\beta=[J(x^+)-K(y)]_+$, the full \emph{fixed-mode} regret is bounded by $\alpha+\beta+\epsilon$. A uniform version uses $\max_i r(h_i,\sigma_i)$ and a uniform upper bound on the anchor-to-raw cost difference. Unrestricted mixed-integer regret needs coverage across the modes or an independent unrestricted lower bound.

\sect{B \quad What terminal-energy correction can certify}
A terminal mismatch can be corrected with one action only when the entire resulting trajectory remains feasible. In a fixed charge/discharge mode, an increment in power magnitude $\Delta p$ for an interval $\Delta t$ changes terminal energy by $\eta_c\Delta p\,\Delta t$ in charge mode and $-\Delta p\,\Delta t/\eta_d$ in discharge mode. With unit retention between periods, the change shifts every subsequent state of charge (SOC) by the same amount, changes that interval's grid import and may use margin at \emph{both} adjacent grid ramps. Correcting only the last SOC value can therefore create a different physical violation.

Let $x$ satisfy the rows before the terminal target changes and $v$ be a chosen unit correction direction. For a positive step $s$, the admissible step along $v$ satisfies
\[
 s \le \min_{i:A_iv>0}\frac{b_i-A_ix}{A_iv}.
\]
If no slope is positive, these rows supply no finite cap. Include power, sign, reserve and availability boxes; all future SOC floors and capacities; import and ramp rows; and every other coupled inequality. Check unaffected equalities and impose the revised terminal equality exactly. This is both necessary and sufficient along that direction in the given affine slice. A successful one-slot certificate therefore has an explicit engineering interpretation. Failure across a finite list of directions does not prove full infeasibility.

\sub{Counterexamples that delimit the mathematics}
Two feasible slots with 1 MWh of headroom apiece can repair a 2 MWh deficit even though every one-slot attempt fails. A boundary anchor on the violated face can also force a full-unit cost change for an arbitrarily small violation. With two rows and two boundary anchors, each anchor can require fraction one while their feasible midpoint permits fraction $2/3$. That last example suggests a way to improve an anchor with certified convex combinations. It supplies no operational method for finding cheap interior points. Costly anchor selection and the per-device slice restriction remain open engineering questions.

\sect{E \quad Does learned continuation improve service?}
On the new primary panel, both the six-hour and twelve-hour continuation controllers shed more flexible load than a simple rule that charges the battery whenever there is spare import capacity. Choosing the comparator on development data changed the conclusion. The W04 six-hour target still served 4.39 MWh/day more than its original forecast rule, and the new learned target served 11.50 MWh/day more than the W04 target. The best eligible six-hour rule served a further 8.05 MWh/day more than the learner.

\sub{What each controller could know and do}
The site has a 35 MW / 180 MWh battery, a 100 MW import cap, a 23 MW/h ramp, a protected 24 MW facility load, and battery charge/discharge efficiencies of 0.94/0.93. It opens with 95 MWh and must close with at least 95 MWh. Each 24-hour trajectory uses current observations and an issued load forecast of up to twelve hours. The shorter horizons receive prefixes of that same forecast. The availability feed discloses outages one hour ahead. The shared reserve calendar can give earlier clues to stress.

W04's 24 development days ranked the original projected-deficit rule, a doubled deficit allowance, and filling to nameplate with spare import capacity at each horizon. All three serve current demand subject to the same device, ramp and reserve limits. Filling won at three, six and twelve hours. It also tied the best H12-information policy that discarded the longer tail. Although eligible for the full forecast, this winning rule only needs the current observation and state.

The rolling LPs shared a solver and first-action tie-break. Each first minimized forecast shedding. The W04 optimizer then protected its calibrated terminal target. The W05 learner estimated the 90th percentile of up to eight hours of subsequent inventory need from 48 W04 training neighbors matched on time and forecast load at the window end. \sect{E \quad How much improvement was available?}
After comparator selection we asked whether \emph{any} feasible policy could have cleared the 0.1 MWh/day practical threshold against fill on these days. For each realized day, construct a feasible fill schedule in rational arithmetic with shedding $U$. Then grant the competing dispatch the complete future load and outage trajectory. Allow simultaneous charging and discharging in its relaxation, while retaining grid, storage, reserve and terminal limits. Any feasible causal controller has shedding at least as large as this relaxed perfect-foresight optimum.

Write the clairvoyant LP as $\min c^\top x$ subject to $Ex=d$, $Ax\le b$ and $\ell\le x\le u$. For any equality multipliers $y$ and inequality multipliers $z\le0$, set $r=c-E^\top y-A^\top z$. A valid lower bound is
\[
 L=d^\top y+b^\top z+\sum_j\min(r_j\ell_j,r_ju_j).
\]
Thus $U-L$ bounds the improvement on that day, even for a competitor with perfect information. HiGHS supplied candidate multipliers. A separate solver-free checker reconstructed the constraints, checked signs and primal feasibility, and evaluated the bound exactly on the stored coefficients.

\sub{What the certificate checks}
Write the minimization relaxation with finite variable boxes and interval rows. A multiplier contributes its selected lower row endpoint if positive and its upper endpoint if negative. If the selected endpoint is unbounded, set that multiplier to zero before computing both the row contribution and the reduced costs. Minimize the remaining reduced-cost linear term over each variable's box. Their sum is a valid lower bound at any such multiplier. The replay uses exact rationals for the stored binary64 coefficients. \sect{C \quad Selective elimination on sparse AC networks}
At a generator bus, real and reactive balance uniquely determine the injections of one pivot generator from all other injections, voltages and branch flows. Substitution removes two variables and retains the pivot's limits as inequalities. Substituting a quadratic generator cost can introduce nonlinear couplings between neighbors. The predeclared score counts missing edges between neighbors and adds $\binom{k}{2}$ for a nonlinear pivot cost, where $k=2+2d+$ number of other generators at the bus and $d$ is the number of distinct neighbors. The guided arm prefers an affine-cost pivot and accepts a score at most six. Alternatives eliminate at every eligible bus, use a cutoff of three incident active branch ends, counting parallel branches separately, or perform a conventional full nonlinear solve.

Four formulations share a complete physical starting tuple. The fifth uses the full equations and conventional DC initialization. All arms use CasADi, IPOPT and the same angle boxes. The independent reviewer reconstructed 25 initial tuples, reevaluated both ends of each active branch, original balances, generator/voltage/angle limits and costs on all 125 final tuples, and rejected 100 deliberate 1 MW corruptions. Independent starts agreed within $2.28\times10^{-10}$ in the maximum coordinate. The registered timer includes input load, selection, model/derivative construction, solve, reconstruction and physical validation. Process startup and post-clock sparsity diagnostics are outside it. Eligibility requires physical checks by 12 s. Matched quality requires a relative objective difference at most $10^{-5}$.

\sect{Admission decisions, provenance and replay}
\textbf{Admit as mathematics.} The minimum segment fraction and its objective identity hold under the full hypotheses in B. They extend the existing AIPC transfer bound once a projection, a feasible lift and a reduced quality certificate exist.
